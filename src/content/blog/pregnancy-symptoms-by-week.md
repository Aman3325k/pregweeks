From the moment of conception, your body begins a complex series of physiological changes to support the new life growing inside you. These adjustments bring a wide array of physical symptoms. While some symptoms—like fatigue, bloating, and mild aches—are standard parts of a healthy gestation, other symptoms require immediate medical attention.

For expectant parents, distinguishing between normal bodily changes and potential warning signs can be stressful. In this guide, we break down what symptoms are normal week-by-week, and list the clinical "red flag" symptoms that require contacting your obstetrician, doctor, or emergency services immediately.

---

## Normal Weekly Symptoms by Trimester

Here is a summary of standard, expected symptoms you may encounter during each phase of pregnancy:

### First Trimester (Weeks 1 to 13)
* **Mild Abdominal Cramping:** As the fertilized egg implants and the uterus begins to stretch, you may feel mild cramping similar to menstrual cramps.
* **Light Spotting:** Known as implantation bleeding, light pink or brown spotting can occur around Weeks 3 or 4. It should not fill a pad.
* **Nausea & Fatigue:** Hormonal surges (hCG and progesterone) trigger morning sickness and drain your energy levels.
* **Breast Tenderness & Frequent Urination:** Increased blood flow and hormonal shifts make breasts sore and cause frequent trips to the bathroom.

### Second Trimester (Weeks 14 to 27)
* **Round Ligament Pain:** Sharp, temporary jabs on one or both sides of the lower abdomen, especially when standing up or coughing, caused by stretching uterine support ligaments.
* **Fetal Hiccups & Movement:** Rhythmic pulsing (hiccups) or soft flutters and kicks (quickening) starting between Weeks 18 and 22.
* **Swelling (Edema):** Mild swelling in the feet, ankles, and hands due to fluid retention and increased pressure on blood vessels in the lower body.
* **Congestion:** Heightened blood flow can lead to nasal stuffiness or occasional mild nosebleeds.

### Third Trimester (Weeks 28 to 40)
* **Braxton Hicks Contractions:** Irregular, painless tightening of the uterus. They do not get closer together and fade when you change positions or drink water.
* **Shortness of Breath:** The growing uterus pushes upward, compressing your diaphragm and reducing lung capacity.
* **Lower Backaches & Pelvic Pressure:** Shifting centers of gravity and the baby's head dropping into the pelvis put strain on your joints.
* **Colostrum Leakage:** Your breasts may leak a thick, yellowish fluid (early milk) in preparation for feeding.

## What is NOT Normal: Red Flag Symptoms to Watch For

While the symptoms above are part of standard gestational physiology, the following "red flag" symptoms indicate potential complications and warrant **immediate contact with your doctor or emergency care:**

### 1. Severe Vaginal Bleeding
* **What's Normal:** Light spotting or pink discharge in early pregnancy, or a small amount of mucus plug/bloody show near delivery.
* **When to Call:** Any bright red bleeding that resembles or is heavier than a menstrual period, bleeding accompanied by severe cramping, or passing tissue. In later trimesters, bleeding can indicate placenta previa or placental abruption, which are medical emergencies.

### 2. Sharp, Severe Abdominal Pain
* **What's Normal:** Mild stretching cramps or temporary round ligament pain.
* **When to Call:** Sharp, persistent, or worsening pain on one side of the lower abdomen, especially in early pregnancy (a warning sign of an ectopic pregnancy). In late pregnancy, severe abdominal pain can indicate placental abruption or preeclampsia.

### 3. Sudden, Severe Swelling & Vision Changes
* **What's Normal:** Gradual, mild swelling in the feet and ankles at the end of the day.
* **When to Call:** Sudden or severe swelling in your hands, face, or around your eyes. Call immediately if this swelling is accompanied by:
  * Persistent, severe headaches that do not respond to acetaminophen.
  * Vision changes (blurred vision, flashing lights, double vision, or temporary blind spots).
  * Sharp pain in the upper right abdomen under the ribs.
  These are classic warning signs of **preeclampsia** (a pregnancy-induced high blood pressure condition), which requires prompt clinical management.

### 4. Decreased Fetal Movement
* **What's Normal:** Soft kicks and rolls that vary throughout the day.
* **When to Call:** Once you reach Week 28, doctors recommend tracking "kick counts." If you notice a sudden decrease in the baby's movements, or if the baby does not move at least 10 times within two hours of focused tracking, contact your healthcare provider immediately for a fetal heart rate check (non-stress test).

### 5. High Fever & Painful Urination
* **What's Normal:** Slight increases in body heat due to high metabolism.
* **When to Call:** A body temperature above 100.4°F (38°C), which can impact fetal development or indicate a systemic infection. Also call if you experience painful, burning urination, cloudy urine, or lower back pain (indicators of a Urinary Tract Infection or kidney infection).

### 6. Persistent Vomiting
* **What's Normal:** Mild to moderate morning sickness where you can still keep some fluids and food down.
* **When to Call:** Inability to keep any liquids or food down for 24 hours, signs of severe dehydration, or losing significant weight (indicators of Hyperemesis Gravidarum).

## When in Doubt, Call Your Provider

The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) and clinical guidelines stress that **it is always better to call your healthcare provider with questions than to wait.** Your OB-GYN, midwife, or clinic team is trained to evaluate symptoms and offer clinical reassurance or direct you to immediate care.
