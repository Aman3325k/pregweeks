Despite its misleading name, morning sickness is one of the most common early signs of pregnancy, affecting up to 80% of expectant mothers. The sudden wave of nausea and vomiting can strike at any hour of the day or night. 

If you are currently struggling to keep food down, your most pressing question is likely: **When is this going to end?**

Fortunately, pregnancy nausea follows a relatively predictable biological timeline. In this guide, we will outline the typical morning sickness timeline based on clinical studies, explain why it happens, and cover evidence-based remedies recommended by the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG).

---

## The Typical Timeline: Week-by-Week

While every pregnancy is unique, most women experience nausea and vomiting within the following timeline:

### Week 4 to 6: The Beginning
For most women, morning sickness begins around **Week 6 of pregnancy** (about two weeks after your missed period). Some highly sensitive individuals may feel mild nausea as early as Week 4 or 5, shortly after implantation. It often starts as a mild, fleeting aversion to certain foods or strong smells (such as coffee, cooking oil, or perfume).

### Week 8 to 10: The Peak
Morning sickness typically **peaks between Weeks 8 and 10**. During this window, the hormone human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) reaches its highest concentration in your bloodstream. Estrogen and progesterone are also rising rapidly. Nausea can be most intense during these weeks, and food aversions are common.

### Week 12 to 14: The Resolution
For the vast majority of expectant mothers, morning sickness begins to improve around **Week 12**. By **Week 14** (the start of the second trimester), symptoms resolve completely for about 90% of women. This coincides with hCG levels leveling off and the placenta taking over hormone production.

### Week 16 and Beyond: Extended Nausea
About 10% of women continue to experience some degree of nausea into the second trimester. In a very small number of cases, mild nausea may persist throughout the entire pregnancy. If your nausea continues past Week 16, discuss it with your healthcare provider to rule out gastrointestinal issues or thyroid conditions.

## Why Does Morning Sickness Happen?

The exact biological cause of morning sickness is not fully understood, but clinical research points to several contributing factors:
* **Skyrocketing hCG:** The timing of morning sickness correlates closely with hCG levels, which double every 48 to 72 hours in early pregnancy.
* **Progesterone Surges:** Progesterone relaxes smooth muscles, including those in the uterus and digestive tract. This slows gastric emptying, leading to acid reflux, bloating, and stomach sensitivity.
* **Heightened Olfactory Senses:** Surge of estrogen makes your sense of smell incredibly sensitive, turning ordinary household scents into sudden triggers.

## Hyperemesis Gravidarum (HG): When It's Severe

While normal morning sickness is uncomfortable, it is generally harmless for you and your baby. However, some women experience a severe form of pregnancy nausea called **Hyperemesis Gravidarum (HG)**. 

HG affects about 0.5% to 2% of pregnancies and is characterized by:
* Severe, persistent vomiting that prevents you from keeping any fluids or food down.
* Weight loss of more than 5% of your pre-pregnancy body weight.
* Signs of dehydration (dark urine, dizziness, extreme thirst, rapid heart rate).
* Electrolyte imbalances.

According to ACOG clinical practice guidelines, HG is a serious medical condition that requires clinical intervention, which may include intravenous (IV) fluids, anti-nausea medications, or dietary therapy. If you display any signs of dehydration or weight loss, contact your doctor immediately.

## Evidence-Based Relief and Remedies

For mild to moderate morning sickness, clinical guidelines and research suggest several lifestyle and dietary changes:

1. **Eat Small, Frequent Meals:** An empty stomach is a major trigger for nausea. Keep simple, bland carbohydrates (like crackers, dry toast, or pretzels) by your bed and eat a few before sitting up in the morning.
2. **Focus on Protein and Complex Carbs:** High-protein snacks (like nuts, yogurt, or hard-boiled eggs) can help stabilize blood sugar and are digested more stably than sugary foods.
3. **Stay Hydrated:** Sip cold fluids throughout the day rather than drinking large amounts at once. Ice-cold water, ginger ale, or electrolyte-rich drinks are often easier to tolerate.
4. **Try Ginger and Peppermint:** Studies show that ginger is a natural anti-nausea remedy. Try ginger tea, ginger candies, or peppermint essential oil diffusers.
5. **Vitamin B6 and Doxylamine:** For persistent nausea, ACOG guidelines recommend **Vitamin B6** (pyridoxine) as a first-line pharmacotherapy option, either alone or combined with doxylamine (an over-the-counter sleep aid). Always consult your doctor before starting any supplements or medications during pregnancy.
