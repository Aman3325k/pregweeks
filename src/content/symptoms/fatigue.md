## What It Is & Why It Happens

Fatigue during pregnancy is a profound, deep physical exhaustion that is very different from standard tiredness. It is a natural biological response to the massive metabolic demands of pregnancy:
1.  **Placental Construction:** In the first trimester, your body is working around the clock to build the **placenta**, the organ that supplies nutrients and oxygen to the fetus. This requires a massive expenditure of maternal energy.
2.  **Hormonal Sedatives:** Levels of **progesterone** rise rapidly. Progesterone acts as a natural central nervous system sedative, making you feel drowsy and slow.
3.  **Cardiovascular Changes:** Your blood volume expands by up to 50% to support the baby. Your heart must pump harder and faster, and your blood pressure often drops in early pregnancy, which can make you feel winded and exhausted.
4.  **Physical Load:** In the third trimester, fatigue returns due to the physical weight of carrying a growing fetus, structural skeletal strains, and sleep disruptions from frequent bathroom trips.

## Typical Timeline

Pregnancy fatigue follows a distinct U-shaped curve:
*   **Weeks 1 to 12:** Fatigue starts very early, often around week 4 or 5, peaking around weeks 8–10 as hormone levels surge. It is one of the most prominent early symptoms.
*   **Weeks 13 to 27:** Fatigue typically **resolves or significantly improves** in the second trimester. This "honeymoon phase" brings a welcome boost of energy.
*   **Weeks 28 to 40:** Fatigue returns in the third trimester as physical load increases and sleep quality declines.

## Safe Comfort Measures

Managing fatigue requires listening to your body and optimizing rest:
*   **Prioritize Sleep:** Aim for 8 to 9 hours of sleep each night. Create a dark, cool, quiet sleep environment.
*   **Take Short Naps:** A 20–30 minute nap in the early afternoon can restore your energy without interfering with nighttime sleep.
*   **Nutritious Diet:** Eat regular, balanced meals containing complex carbohydrates, lean proteins, and **iron-rich foods** (such as spinach, beans, and lean meats). Anemia (low iron) can exacerbate exhaustion.
*   **Gentle Exercise:** While it seems counterintuitive, light physical activity like a 20-minute daily walk can stimulate circulation and boost daytime energy levels.
*   **Hydrate Early:** Drink plenty of fluids during the day, but limit liquid intake after 8:00 PM to minimize nighttime bathroom trips.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

While tiredness is normal, severe or constant fatigue can indicate a medical concern. Contact your doctor if you experience:
*   Extreme, constant fatigue that does not improve with rest.
*   Fatigue accompanied by pale skin, shortness of breath, dizziness, or chest pain (signs of **pregnancy anemia**).
*   Persistent feelings of sadness, anxiety, hopelessness, or loss of interest in activities you normally enjoy (indicators of **prenatal depression**, which requires clinical support).
*   Fatigue accompanied by severe headaches, swelling, or rapid weight gain.
