## What It Is & Why It Happens

Shortness of breath, or gestational dyspnea, affects up to 75% of pregnant individuals. While it can feel alarming, it is typically a normal response to the maternal body's physiological adaptations.

In early pregnancy (first trimester), breathlessness is driven by **progesterone**. Progesterone acts directly on the brain's respiratory center, increasing both the depth and frequency of your breaths. This allows your body to extract more oxygen from the air and expel more carbon dioxide, optimizing gas exchange across the placenta for the embryo.

In late pregnancy (third trimester), the primary cause is **physical compression**. The growing uterus expands upward into the abdominal cavity, pushing the stomach, intestines, and liver against the diaphragm. This elevates the diaphragm by approximately 4 centimeters, limiting the lungs' ability to expand fully and causing a sensation of shallow breathing or chest crowding.

## Typical Timeline

Gestational dyspnea can occur throughout pregnancy, but its triggers evolve:
*   **First & Second Trimesters (Weeks 4-27):** Primarily hormonal. You may notice mild breathlessness during speech or light activity as your body adjusts to elevated progesterone levels.
*   **Third Trimester (Weeks 28-36):** Peak physical compression. Breathlessness is most noticeable when sitting, bending over, or climbing stairs due to the physical size of the uterus.
*   **Late Third Trimester (Weeks 37-40):** Often improves as the baby drops into the pelvis (lightening), creating space beneath your ribs.

## Safe Comfort Measures

You can improve comfort and support healthy breathing with these safe steps:
*   **Practice Good Posture:** Stand and sit up straight to give your lungs as much room to expand as possible. Avoid slouching.
*   **Sleep Elevated:** Prop yourself up with extra pillows or use a pregnancy wedge pillow to sleep at a slight incline, which reduces pressure on the diaphragm.
*   **Pace Yourself:** Listen to your body. Break up activities into manageable tasks and rest when you feel tired.
*   **Use Gentle Breathing Exercises:** Try chest breathing or purse-lipped breathing to calm your nervous system and expand lung capacity.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

While mild breathlessness is standard, **severe, sudden, or worsening shortness of breath can indicate serious underlying medical conditions** (such as asthma, severe anemia, pulmonary embolism, or preeclampsia).

**Seek immediate medical attention or call emergency services if you experience:**
*   Sudden, severe shortness of breath that starts abruptly.
*   Chest pain, pressure, tightness, or pain when breathing in.
*   Coughing up blood or pink-tinged, frothy sputum.
*   Wheezing, coughing, or a history of asthma that is worsening.
*   Bluish color on your lips, fingers, or toes (cyanosis).
*   A rapid, pounding heartbeat or feeling faint, dizzy, or lightheaded.
*   Shortness of breath accompanied by sudden swelling in your face or hands, or severe calf pain.
