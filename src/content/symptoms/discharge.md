## What It Is & Why It Happens

An increase in vaginal discharge, clinically referred to as **leukorrhea**, is one of the earliest and most persistent signs of pregnancy. This thin, milky white, mild-smelling or odorless discharge is entirely normal and serves a vital protective purpose.

During pregnancy, surging levels of estrogen increase blood flow to the pelvic area, stimulating the vaginal mucosa and cervix to produce more secretions. This mechanism is designed to help keep the birth canal clean, flushing away bacteria and preventing pathogens from ascending into the uterus. As gestation progresses, the cervix also forms a thick mucus barrier (the mucus plug) to seal the uterine opening, protecting the developing fetus from external infection.

## Typical Timeline

Leukorrhea usually begins in the **first trimester** (around Week 4 to 6) and gradually increases in volume as pregnancy advances. 
*   **First Trimester (Weeks 1-12):** Minor increase in thin, clear or white secretions.
*   **Second Trimester (Weeks 13-27):** Discharge becomes noticeably heavier as estrogen levels rise.
*   **Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40):** Peak discharge volume. Close to your due date (Weeks 37-40), you may notice a thick, mucus-like discharge tinged with pink or brown blood. This is the mucus plug (or "bloody show"), indicating that the cervix is softening and dilating in preparation for labor.

## Safe Comfort Measures

While increased discharge is normal, staying comfortable and dry is important:
*   **Practice Good Hygiene:** Keep the perineal area clean and dry. Always wipe from front to back after using the restroom to prevent introducing rectal bacteria into the vagina.
*   **Wear Breathable Underwear:** Choose breathable cotton undergarments and avoid tight-fitting pants or synthetic fabrics that trap moisture.
*   **Use Panty Liners:** If the discharge is heavy, wear unscented panty liners. Do not use tampons, as they can introduce harmful bacteria into the birth canal and cause irritation.
*   **Avoid Douching:** Never douche during pregnancy. Douching disrupts the vaginal microbiome, increases the risk of bacterial vaginosis (BV) or yeast infections, and can push bacteria higher into the reproductive tract.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

Although leukorrhea is benign, changes in color, consistency, or odor can indicate infections or pregnancy complications that require clinical evaluation.

**Contact your healthcare provider if you experience:**
*   **Amniotic Fluid Leak:** A sudden gush or a continuous, uncontrollable trickle of thin, watery fluid (which may indicate premature rupture of membranes).
*   **Signs of Infection:** Discharge that is green, yellow, thick and clumpy (like cottage cheese), or gray, especially if accompanied by a strong fishy odor.
*   **Vaginal Discomfort:** Severe itching, burning during urination, swelling, or redness in the vulvar area.
*   **Preterm Labor Indicators:** A sudden change in discharge type (becoming watery, mucus-like, or bloody) accompanied by cramping or contractions before Week 37.
