## What It Is & Why It Happens

Frequent urination is characterized by the need to urinate more often than usual, sometimes waking up multiple times during the night (nocturia). It is a classic early sign of pregnancy and typically recurs in the final weeks before delivery.

In the **first trimester**, the primary cause is hormonal. Surging levels of **human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)** and progesterone increase blood flow throughout the body, including to the kidneys. The kidneys work more efficiently to filter out waste, resulting in rapid bladder filling. Additionally, the growing uterus, though still small, is located directly behind the bladder and begins to press against it.

In the **third trimester**, the trigger is physical. The fetus grows much larger and descends into the pelvic cavity (a process called lightening), pressing directly on top of the bladder and restricting its capacity.

## Typical Timeline

Frequent urination follows a U-shaped pattern:
*   **First Trimester (Weeks 4-12):** Very common due to hormonal surges and early uterine changes.
*   **Second Trimester (Weeks 13-27):** Often improves because the uterus rises out of the pelvic cavity and into the abdomen, relieving direct pressure on the bladder.
*   **Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40):** Returns with intensity as the baby grows larger and drops into the pelvis in preparation for birth.

## Safe Comfort Measures

While you cannot stop frequent urination, you can manage it safely:
*   **Lean Forward:** When urinating, lean forward slightly. This helps empty your bladder completely, reducing the frequency of trips.
*   **Avoid Diuretics:** Avoid drinks containing caffeine (like coffee, soda, or tea), which act as diuretics and stimulate the bladder.
*   **Time Your Hydration:** Drink the majority of your daily fluids during the daytime, and taper off in the evening to reduce sleep disruptions.
*   **Strengthen Pelvic Floor:** Perform daily **Kegel exercises** to strengthen your pelvic floor muscles, supporting bladder control and preventing stress leaks.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

Frequent urination is normal, but **it can be confused with a urinary tract infection (UTI).** Pregnant individuals are highly susceptible to UTIs, which, if left untreated, can progress to kidney infections and trigger preterm labor.

**Contact your healthcare provider immediately if you experience:**
*   Pain or a burning sensation during urination.
*   An urge to urinate accompanied by passing only a few drops.
*   Blood in your urine (pink, red, or cloudy urine).
*   Foul-smelling urine.
*   Fever, chills, or lower back/side pain near the kidneys.
*   Inability to urinate at all (bladder retention).
