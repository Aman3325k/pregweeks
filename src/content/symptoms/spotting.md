## What It Is & Why It Happens

Spotting is defined as light vaginal bleeding that is typically pink, brown, or light red, and does not require the use of a sanitary pad (a panty liner is usually sufficient). Up to 25% of pregnant individuals experience some degree of light spotting during the first trimester. 

In early pregnancy, the primary trigger is **implantation bleeding**, which occurs as the fertilized egg burrows into the vascular lining of the uterus, approximately 10 to 14 days after conception. Another common cause is increased **cervical vascularity**. Due to surging estrogen and progesterone levels, the blood supply to the cervix increases dramatically. Consequently, the cervical cells become highly sensitive, making minor spotting common after a pelvic examination, cervical swab, or sexual intercourse. Other benign causes include minor cervical polyps or subchorionic irritation.

## Typical Timeline

Spotting is most common during the **first trimester**, specifically between **Weeks 4 and 12**. Implantation spotting typically happens around Week 4 (coinciding with the expected menstrual period). Spotting related to cervical sensitivity can occur intermittently throughout the first and second trimesters. In the third trimester, spotting may indicate cervical changes, such as the loss of the mucus plug (the "bloody show") near Week 37-40, signaling that labor is approaching.

## Safe Comfort Measures

If you experience minor spotting, follow these self-care guidelines:
*   **Rest and Elevate:** Reduce strenuous activities, avoid heavy lifting, and take time to rest on your side.
*   **Avoid Cervical Penetration:** Abstain from sexual intercourse and do not use tampons or douches until you have been evaluated and cleared by your healthcare provider.
*   **Monitor and Document:** Wear a panty liner to track the color (pink, brown, bright red), consistency, and volume of the discharge. Note any accompanying symptoms like cramping.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

While light spotting is often benign, **vaginal bleeding during pregnancy always requires clinical evaluation.** It is vital to distinguish mild spotting from red-flag symptoms that could indicate ectopic pregnancy, early pregnancy loss (miscarriage), or placental issues.

**Contact your healthcare provider immediately or go to the nearest emergency room if you experience:**
*   **Heavy Bleeding:** Vaginal bleeding that is bright red and heavy enough to soak through a sanitary pad in an hour or less.
*   **Severe Pain:** Sharp, persistent, or one-sided pelvic or abdominal pain.
*   **Systemic Symptoms:** Dizziness, lightheadedness, fainting, fever, or chills.
*   **Shoulder Pain:** Sharp pain at the tip of your shoulder, which can be a primary sign of internal bleeding from a ruptured ectopic pregnancy.
*   **Tissue Passage:** Passing clots or tissue from the vagina (save the tissue in a clean container if possible for your doctor to evaluate).
