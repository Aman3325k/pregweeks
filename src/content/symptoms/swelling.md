## What It Is & Why It Happens

Swelling, or gestational edema, occurs when excess fluid accumulates in the body's tissues. During pregnancy, your blood volume increases by approximately 50%, and your body produces more fluids to support fetal development and prepare pelvic joints for birth.

As the pregnancy progresses, the growing uterus places physical pressure on the **inferior vena cava**, the large vein that returns blood from the lower limbs back to the heart. This compression, combined with the relaxing effect of progesterone on vein walls, slows down circulation. Consequently, fluid pools in the lower extremities (feet, ankles, and calves) and hands, particularly during hot weather, after prolonged standing, or at the end of the day.

## Typical Timeline

Gestational swelling is progressive:
*   **First Trimester (Weeks 1-12):** Rare. Minor bloating may occur due to progesterone, but physical tissue swelling is uncommon.
*   **Second Trimester (Weeks 13-27):** Begins to appear, particularly in the feet and hands toward the end of the trimester (Weeks 20-27).
*   **Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40):** Peak intensity. Mild swelling in the ankles, feet, and fingers is extremely common as fetal weight reaches its maximum.

## Safe Comfort Measures

You can minimize mild edema and support healthy circulation with these safe remedies:
*   **Elevate Your Feet:** Whenever you sit or lie down, prop your legs up above the level of your heart using pillows to encourage fluid drainage.
*   **Stay Hydrated:** Drink 8 to 10 glasses of water daily. Limiting fluids does not reduce swelling; rather, it worsens fluid retention.
*   **Wear Compression Socks:** Put on supportive maternity compression stockings in the morning before standing up to support vein return.
*   **Avoid Prolonged Sitting or Standing:** Change positions frequently. Take short walks to activate the calf muscles, which act as natural pumps.
*   **Sleep on Your Left Side:** This position removes pressure from the inferior vena cava, optimizing blood flow and kidney function.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

While mild swelling is expected, **sudden or asymmetric swelling can indicate serious clinical complications.**

**Contact your healthcare provider immediately or seek emergency medical care if you notice:**
*   **Sudden Swelling:** Rapid, noticeable swelling in your face, hands, or puffiness around your eyes, especially if it appears overnight. This is a primary indicator of **preeclampsia**.
*   **Asymmetric Leg Swelling:** Swelling that is significantly worse in one leg than the other, particularly if accompanied by pain, tenderness, warmth, or redness in the calf. This can indicate a **deep vein thrombosis (DVT)** (a dangerous blood clot).
*   Swelling accompanied by a severe, persistent headache, visual disturbances, or shortness of breath.
