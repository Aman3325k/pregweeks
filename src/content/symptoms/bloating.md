## What It Is & Why It Happens

Abdominal bloating and gas are extremely common and expected physical symptoms throughout pregnancy. They are characterized by a feeling of abdominal fullness, pressure, distension, and flatulence.

The primary cause of gestational bloating is **progesterone**. Progesterone is essential for maintaining a healthy pregnancy because it relaxes smooth muscle tissues throughout the body, including the uterus (preventing premature contractions). However, this hormone also relaxes the smooth muscles of the gastrointestinal tract. Consequently, the passage of food through the stomach and intestines slows down significantly. 

As digestion slows, bacteria in the digestive tract have more time to break down food, resulting in increased gas production. In the third trimester, the physical mass of the uterus presses directly on the intestines, compounding this effect and causing persistent bloating.

## Typical Timeline

Bloating and gas are progressive:
*   **First Trimester (Weeks 4-12):** Begins early. Driven entirely by the initial hormonal surge of progesterone. Many individuals experience abdominal distension before they have a visible baby bump.
*   **Second Trimester (Weeks 13-27):** Often continues, though it may temporarily improve as hormonal levels stabilize.
*   **Third Trimester (Weeks 28-40):** Reaches its peak due to the combined effects of progesterone and the physical size of the uterus crowding the abdominal organs.

## Safe Comfort Measures

You can safely reduce bloating and support active digestion with these lifestyle adjustments:
*   **Eat Small, Frequent Meals:** Consuming five or six small meals instead of three large ones prevents overloading the digestive system.
*   **Chew Thoroughly & Eat Slowly:** Chewing food completely prevents swallowing excess air, which is a primary source of gas.
*   **Stay Hydrated:** Drink 8 to 10 glasses of water daily. Hydration keeps the digestive tract moving and prevents constipation (which worsens bloating).
*   **Exercise Daily:** Take a gentle 15-to-20 minute walk after meals. Physical movement stimulates intestinal motility.
*   **Avoid Trigger Foods:** Limit gas-producing vegetables (beans, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage), fried or greasy foods, carbonated drinks, and chewing gum.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

While standard bloating is benign, persistent abdominal pain or signs of bowel obstruction require clinical evaluation.

**Contact your healthcare provider immediately if you experience:**
*   Severe, sharp, or constant abdominal pain or cramping.
*   Severe constipation or inability to pass gas or bowel movements for several days.
*   Severe, persistent diarrhea or blood in your stool.
*   Frequent vomiting, nausea, or fever accompanying the bloating.
*   Bloating accompanied by regular uterine contractions before Week 37.
