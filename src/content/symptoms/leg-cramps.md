## What It Is & Why It Happens

**Leg cramps** are sudden, involuntary, painful contractions of the calf muscles, ankles, or feet. They affect up to 45% of pregnant individuals and most commonly occur at night, waking you from sleep.

The exact cause of gestational leg cramps is not fully understood, but several physiological factors contribute:
*   **Muscle Fatigue:** The calf muscles work harder during the day to support your expanding body weight and adapt to changes in your center of gravity.
*   **Venous Stasis (Sluggish Circulation):** The growing uterus places physical pressure on the pelvic veins and the inferior vena cava (the large vein returning blood from the lower body to the heart). This slows down circulation, leading to fluid retention and muscle cramping.
*   **Mineral Imbalance:** Changes in how your body processes minerals (calcium, magnesium, and phosphorus) can affect muscle contraction and nerve signals.

## Typical Timeline

Leg cramps typically begin in the **second trimester** (around **Week 20**) and can increase in frequency and intensity during the **third trimester** (Weeks 28-40) as fetal weight increases and pelvic pressure peaks. They are most common during the second half of pregnancy and resolve quickly after delivery.

## Safe Comfort Measures

You can safely relieve and prevent leg cramps with these physical remedies:
*   **Flex Your Foot:** When a cramp starts, stretch the calf muscle immediately by straightening your leg and flexing your foot upward (toes toward your shin). Avoid pointing your toes, as this can worsen the cramp.
*   **Stay Active:** Perform gentle stretching exercises before bed, such as calf stretches against a wall. Take a short walk daily to support healthy circulation.
*   **Hydrate & Eat Balanced Meals:** Drink 8 to 10 glasses of water daily. Ensure your diet includes foods rich in calcium (pasteurized dairy, leafy greens) and magnesium (nuts, seeds, whole grains).
*   **Apply Heat or Massage:** Take a warm bath, use a warm compress, or gently massage the cramped muscle to promote relaxation.
*   **Elevate Your Legs:** Prop your feet up on pillows when sitting or resting to help return blood to your heart.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

While occasional cramps in both legs are common, pain in only one leg accompanied by swelling or redness can indicate a deep vein blood clot.

**Contact your healthcare provider immediately or seek emergency medical care if you experience:**
*   **Signs of DVT (Deep Vein Thrombosis):** Constant, localized pain, swelling, tenderness, warmth, or redness in only one calf or thigh (asymmetric leg swelling). A DVT is a medical emergency that can lead to a pulmonary embolism.
*   Severe muscle pain that does not improve after stretching, rest, or hydration.
*   Difficulty walking or localized numbness and tingling in your leg.
