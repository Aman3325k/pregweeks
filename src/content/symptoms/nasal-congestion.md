## What It Is & Why It Happens

**Nasal Congestion**, specifically **pregnancy rhinitis**, is a common respiratory symptom during pregnancy, affecting up to 30% of pregnant individuals. It is characterized by a persistent stuffy nose, congestion, or postnasal drip that occurs in the absence of a viral infection or allergies.

The primary cause of pregnancy rhinitis is the surge of **estrogen**. 

Estrogen stimulates mucus production and causes the blood vessels throughout your body to dilate and expand. The blood vessels lining your nasal passages are particularly sensitive to these hormonal shifts. The increased blood flow and vasodilation cause the nasal membranes to swell, narrowing the nasal airways and making it difficult to breathe through your nose. 

Increased blood volume (which rises by 50% during pregnancy) also increases pressure inside these delicate nasal capillaries, contributing to swelling and congestion.

## Typical Timeline

Pregnancy rhinitis can begin as early as the first trimester, but it most commonly starts during the **second trimester** (around **Week 12**) and can persist throughout the **third trimester** (up to Week 40). It typically resolves completely within one to two weeks postpartum as estrogen levels drop and blood volume returns to pre-pregnancy levels.

## Safe Comfort Measures

You can safely relieve nasal congestion and keep nasal passages clear with these non-medicinal remedies:
*   **Saline Nasal Sprays and Rinses:** Use over-the-counter preservative-free saline nasal sprays or a neti pot (using distilled or boiled water) to flush out mucus and hydrate dry nasal tissues.
*   **Elevate Your Head:** Sleep with your head elevated on extra pillows to help drain fluid from your sinuses and nasal passages, reducing congestion at night.
*   **Use a Cool-Mist Humidifier:** Run a cool-mist humidifier in your bedroom at night to keep the air moist, which prevents the nasal membranes from drying out and cracking.
*   **Inhale Steam:** Take a warm shower or sit in a steamy bathroom for 10 to 15 minutes to help loosen thick mucus.
*   **Drink Plenty of Fluids:** Stay well-hydrated by drinking water, warm tea, or clear broths. Hydration thins mucus, making it easier to clear.
*   **Nasal Strips:** Apply external adhesive nasal strips across the bridge of your nose before bed to physically pull open your nasal passages, improving airflow.
*   **Gentle Exercise:** Engage in light, doctor-approved physical activity (like walking), which naturally constricts nasal blood vessels and can temporarily relieve congestion.

## When to Call the Doctor (Red Flags)

While pregnancy rhinitis is benign, nasal symptoms can sometimes indicate infections that require treatment.

**Contact your healthcare provider if you experience:**
*   Nasal congestion accompanied by a fever, facial pain, pressure around your eyes, or thick green/yellow nasal discharge, which are signs of a **sinus infection**.
*   Congestion accompanied by a persistent sore throat, body aches, a deep cough, or wheezing (possible viral or bacterial respiratory infection).
*   Congestion that makes sleep impossible, causing severe daytime exhaustion or worsening symptoms of sleep apnea.
*   A stuffy nose accompanied by sudden swelling in your face/hands, a severe headache, or vision changes (preeclampsia indicators).
*   Frequent nasal bleeding that does not stop after 20 minutes of direct pressure.
