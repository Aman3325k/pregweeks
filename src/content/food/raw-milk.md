## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

Raw, unpasteurized milk and any dairy products made from it (such as certain soft cheeses, creams, and yogurts) should be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

According to standard warnings from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, raw milk is a high-risk source of dangerous foodborne pathogens. Unlike pasteurized dairy, raw milk has not undergone the pasteurization heating process, which is designed to destroy harmful bacteria. 

The primary pathogen of concern in raw milk is **Listeria monocytogenes**. Listeria infection (listeriosis) in pregnant individuals can cross the placental barrier, leading to miscarriage, premature delivery, stillbirth, or life-threatening systemic infections in newborns. Other dangerous bacteria commonly found in raw milk include **Salmonella**, **Escherichia coli (E. coli)**, and **Campylobacter**, all of which can cause severe gastrointestinal illness and dehydration.

## Nutritional Benefits of Safe Alternatives

Dairy is a vital component of prenatal nutrition. It provides high-quality **calcium** necessary for the development of your baby’s bones and teeth, along with **protein, phosphorus, and vitamin D**. 

To enjoy these benefits safely, choose commercially pasteurized milks. The pasteurization process heats milk to **161°F (71.7°C)** for at least 15 seconds, killing pathogens while preserving all essential nutrients. Safe alternatives include:
*   **Pasteurized Cow's or Goat's Milk:** Highly nutritious and safe.
*   **Fortified Plant Milks:** Almond, soy, oat, or pea protein milks fortified with calcium and vitamin D.
*   **Pasteurized Yogurt and Kefir:** Probiotic-rich options that support gut health.

## Safe Consumption & Kitchen Hygiene

To ensure dairy safety in your daily routine, follow these precautions:
*   **Check Every Label:** Read the ingredients list and packaging markers on all liquid dairy, whipping cream, eggnogs, and soft cheeses. If it does not explicitly state "pasteurized," do not consume it.
*   **Proper Refrigeration:** Keep your refrigerator temperature set at or below **40°F (4°C)** to slow any potential bacterial growth.
*   **Discard Expired Items:** Consume pasteurized milk within 7 days of opening, and throw away any items that have passed their expiration date.
