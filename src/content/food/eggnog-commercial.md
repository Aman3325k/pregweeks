## Safety Status: Generally Safe

Store-bought, commercial eggnog is **safe** to consume during pregnancy, provided it is non-alcoholic.

According to guidelines from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, commercial eggnog is undergo commercial pasteurization. Pasteurization heats the milk, cream, and egg mixture to a high temperature (typically **161°F / 71.7°C** for at least 15 seconds) to destroy foodborne pathogens, including **Salmonella enteritidis** and **Listeria monocytogenes**. 

Because of this rigorous heat-treatment process, commercial eggnog does not carry the bacterial risks associated with raw eggs used in traditional homemade eggnog.

## Nutritional Profile & Considerations

While safe from pathogens, commercial eggnog is a rich, calorie-dense beverage:
*   **Calcium & Protein:** Made from milk and cream, it provides calcium essential for your baby’s bones and protein for tissue growth.
*   **High Sugar & Fat Content:** Commercial eggnog is high in added sugars and saturated fats. If you are managing gestational diabetes or monitoring weight gain, consume it in moderation as a seasonal treat.
*   **Choline & Vitamin D:** Contains trace amounts of choline from the pasteurized egg yolk, supporting fetal brain health.

## Safe Selection and Kitchen Storage

To enjoy commercial eggnog safely:
*   **Confirm Pasteurization:** Double-check the product label for the word "pasteurized." Virtually all commercial dairy brands sold in major supermarkets are pasteurized.
*   **Verify Alcohol Content:** Ensure you are purchasing regular non-alcoholic eggnog. Avoid pre-mixed alcoholic eggnogs sold in liquor sections, as alcohol is contraindicated during pregnancy.
*   **Keep Cold:** Store commercial eggnog in your refrigerator at or below **40°F (4°C)**.
*   **Consume Promptly:** Drink opened commercial eggnog within 5 to 7 days, and discard any beverage that has passed its expiration date.
