## Safety Status: Safe (When Well-Cooked)

Cooked salmon is classified under the **safe** category and is widely considered one of the best seafood choices for pregnant individuals. 

According to guidelines from the **American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG)**, the FDA, and the EPA, salmon is exceptionally low in mercury. This allows expectant mothers to consume it regularly—up to two to three servings (8 to 12 ounces) per week—without risk of heavy metal toxicity. 

However, this safety status applies only to **fully cooked** salmon. Raw salmon (such as sashimi or raw rolls) must be avoided due to potential exposure to parasites (like tapeworms) and pathogenic bacteria. Similarly, refrigerated cold-smoked salmon (lox) should be avoided because the cold-smoking process does not reliably kill **Listeria monocytogenes**, unless the salmon is cooked until steaming hot in a dish like a casserole or quiche.

## Nutritional Benefits: The Power of Omega-3s

Salmon is a nutritional powerhouse for prenatal health. Its most significant benefit is its concentration of **docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)** and **eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA)**, which are long-chain Omega-3 fatty acids. 
*   **Brain and Eye Development:** DHA is a major structural component of the human brain and retina. Consuming adequate DHA during pregnancy is directly linked to improved cognitive development, visual acuity, and motor coordination in infants.
*   **High-Quality Protein:** Supports the rapid cell growth of both the placenta and the fetus.
*   **Vitamin D & Calcium:** Salmon is one of the few natural food sources of vitamin D, which helps your body absorb calcium to build your baby's bones.

## Safe Preparation & Consuming Guidelines

To maximize the benefits of salmon safely:
*   **Verify Internal Temperature:** Cook salmon until the internal temperature reaches **145°F (63°C)**, or until the flesh is completely opaque and flakes easily with a fork.
*   **Store Properly:** Keep fresh salmon in the refrigerator at or below 40°F (4°C) and cook it within one to two days of purchase.
*   **Prevent Cross-Contamination:** Keep raw salmon separate from produce and ready-to-eat foods in your shopping cart and refrigerator. Always wash hands, cutting boards, and utensils with hot, soapy water after handling raw fish.
