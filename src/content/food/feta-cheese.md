## Safety Status: Safe if Pasteurized

Feta cheese is generally **safe** to consume during pregnancy, with one critical condition: **it must be made from pasteurized milk**. 

According to official warnings and guidelines from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, soft cheeses (including feta, brie, camembert, and blue cheese) present a high risk of *Listeria monocytogenes* contamination if made with unpasteurized raw milk. Pasteurization is a heating process that destroys these harmful bacteria, making pasteurized feta completely safe to eat. 

If feta is imported, bought at a local farm, or purchased at a specialty cheese shop, it may be made from raw, unpasteurized milk. Unpasteurized feta must be strictly avoided during pregnancy due to the risk of listeriosis, a serious foodborne illness that can cross the placenta and lead to miscarriage, stillbirth, or neonatal infection.

## Nutritional Benefits of Safe Feta

When pasteurized, feta cheese is a nutritious addition to a prenatal diet. It provides:
*   **Calcium:** Vital for the development and maintenance of your baby’s bones, teeth, and cardiovascular system.
*   **Protein:** Supports the growth of fetal tissues and the placenta.
*   **Vitamin B12 & Zinc:** Essential for red blood cell formation and immune function.
*   **Probiotics:** Pasteurized feta can contain beneficial bacteria that support maternal gut health and digestion.

## Safe Consumption & Kitchen Hygiene

To safely enjoy feta cheese in your salads, wraps, and cooked dishes:
*   **Verify the Label:** Confirm the word "pasteurized" on the product packaging. In the United States, federal law requires pasteurization for all domestic soft cheeses, but imported cheeses should be checked carefully.
*   **Avoid Salad Bars:** Skip crumbled feta from open salad bars or buffets, as these environments are prone to cross-contamination.
*   **Store Properly:** Keep feta sealed in its brine and store it in the refrigerator at or below **40°F (4°C)**. Consume it within one week of opening.
*   **Cook for Extra Safety:** Heating feta cheese until it is bubbling hot (such as on a baked pasta or pizza) eliminates any potential bacterial risk, making even questionable soft cheeses safe to eat.
