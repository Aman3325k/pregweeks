## Safety Status: Safe (Fully Cooked)

Fully cooked **clams** are **safe** and highly beneficial to consume during pregnancy. 

While raw clams are strictly forbidden due to high risks of foodborne infections, thorough cooking at high temperatures destroys harmful pathogens, including **Vibrio bacteria**, **Norovirus**, **Hepatitis A**, and **Salmonella**.

The **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** state that cooking shellfish to an internal temperature of **145°F (63°C)** renders it safe for pregnant individuals. 

Because clams are small and low in the marine food chain, they also have very low levels of methylmercury, making them an excellent choice for prenatal seafood consumption.

## Nutritional Benefits

Cooked clams provide several essential nutrients that support maternal health and fetal development:
*   **Outstanding Iron Source:** Iron is vital for producing hemoglobin, which carries oxygen to your tissues and your baby. During pregnancy, maternal blood volume increases by 50%, raising iron needs. Clams offer one of the highest concentrations of heme iron (the most easily absorbed form) of any food.
*   **High Vitamin B12:** Essential for fetal brain development, nervous system function, and DNA synthesis.
*   **Zinc and Selenium:** Trace minerals that support cellular division, immune function, and thyroid health.
*   **Lean Protein:** Aids in the building of fetal tissues and organs.

## Safe Preparation and Cooking Guidelines

To enjoy cooked clams safely, adhere to these guidelines:
*   **Discard Closed Shells:** When steaming or boiling whole clams, discard any clams that fail to open after cooking. A shell that remains closed indicates the clam was dead before cooking, which increases the risk of bacterial decomposition and heat-stable toxins.
*   **Cook Thoroughly:** Steam or boil whole clams until the shells open, then continue cooking for another **4 to 5 minutes** in steam or boiling water. For shucked clams, boil for at least **3 minutes** or fry at **375°F (190°C)** for at least **3 minutes**.
*   **Portion Limits:** Clams are on the FDA's "Best Choices" list. You can safely consume **8 to 12 ounces (2 to 3 servings) per week** of low-mercury seafood, including cooked clams.
