## Safety Status: Generally Safe

Commercially pasteurized eggs (both in-shell and liquid) are completely **safe** to consume during pregnancy, even in raw or gently cooked forms.

According to guidelines from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, pasteurization effectively eliminates the risk of **Salmonella enteritidis** contamination. The pasteurization process involves heating in-shell eggs to a specific temperature for a precise duration. This heat-treatment kills any Salmonella bacteria inside the egg without cooking the egg white or yolk, making it safe for recipes that require raw or undercooked egg products.

In contrast, unpasteurized eggs must always be cooked until both the white and the yolk are completely firm to eliminate Salmonella risk.

## Nutritional Benefits for Pregnancy

Eggs are a powerhouse of prenatal nutrition, and pasteurized eggs allow you to enjoy them safely:
*   **Choline:** Essential for fetal brain development and helping prevent neural tube defects. One large egg provides about 150 mg of choline, satisfying a significant portion of the daily requirement.
*   **High-Quality Protein:** Supports the rapid growth of fetal tissues, the placenta, and maternal blood volume expansion.
*   **Lutein & Zeaxanthin:** Carotenoids that support the development of fetal vision and eye health.
*   **Vitamin D & Iron:** Supports healthy skeletal development and red blood cell production, preventing maternal anemia.

## Safe Usage & Kitchen Storage

To utilize pasteurized eggs safely in your kitchen:
*   **Verify the Label:** Ensure the carton is explicitly labeled "pasteurized." Look for the red "P" stamp on the shells of in-shell pasteurized eggs.
*   **Use in Raw-Egg Recipes:** Pasteurized eggs are the only safe option for making homemade eggnog, Caesar salad dressing, mayonnaise, hollandaise sauce, tiramisu, or meringue during pregnancy.
*   **Refrigerate Promptly:** Keep pasteurized eggs stored in their original carton in the main body of the refrigerator at or below **40°F (4°C)**. Do not store them in the refrigerator door.
*   **Handle Raw Batter Safely:** While pasteurized eggs remove the Salmonella risk, raw flour in cake batter or cookie dough can still harbor E. coli. Always use heat-treated flour if making edible raw cookie dough.
