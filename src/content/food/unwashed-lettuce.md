## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

Unwashed lettuce, unwashed spinach, kale, and other raw salad greens must be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

While leafy greens are highly nutritious, eating them unwashed is a primary cause of foodborne parasitic and bacterial infections. The **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** and the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** emphasize that raw agricultural products must be washed thoroughly under running water to remove harmful contaminants before they are safe to eat.

## Pathogens in Soil and Fetal Risks

Leafy greens grow close to the ground, where they can easily contact soil, animal feces, or contaminated irrigation water:

*   **Toxoplasma gondii:** This parasite is a major YMYL concern. Cats are the primary hosts, shedding millions of oocysts in their feces, which can survive in soil for months. If greens are grown in contaminated soil and eaten unwashed, the mother can contract Toxoplasmosis. This parasite can cross the placenta, causing miscarriage, stillbirth, or severe congenital neurological damage, hydrocephalus, and visual impairment in the baby.
*   **Escherichia coli (E. coli) and Salmonella:** Contaminated water runoff from nearby livestock can transfer these bacteria to lettuce crops. E. coli and Salmonella cause severe food poisoning, resulting in high fever, vomiting, and dehydration, which can trigger uterine contractions.
*   **Listeria monocytogenes:** Listeria can reside in soil and water. Because it is resilient and grows at cold temperatures, raw unwashed greens can become vectors for listeriosis, leading to premature labor or systemic newborn infections.

## Safe Preparation and Washing Guidelines

To ensure your salad greens are safe:

*   **Wash Under Running Water:** Rinse all lettuce leaves thoroughly under clean, running tap water. Do not soak lettuce in a filled sink or bowl, as this can spread contaminants from one leaf to another. Running water physically dislodges soil and bacteria.
*   **Scrub the Rind:** If prepping leafy vegetables with outer skins (like cabbage), discard the outermost leaves before washing the rest of the head.
*   **Dry and Store Properly:** After washing, dry the greens with a clean paper towel or a salad spinner. Store washed greens in a clean container in the refrigerator at or below **40°F (4°C)**.
*   **Handle Pre-washed Bags with Caution:** While pre-washed bagged salads are convenient, they are linked to multiple Listeria and E. coli recalls. Washing them again under running water is a simple precaution.
