## Safety Status: Safe to Consume

Commercially manufactured, store-bought mayonnaise is entirely **safe** to consume during pregnancy. 

Unlike homemade mayonnaise, which is typically made with raw egg yolks and carries a high risk of foodborne illness, the commercial food industry is subject to strict pasteurization regulations. According to the **U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, commercially packaged mayonnaise is made using pasteurized eggs, making it safe from *Salmonella* and other foodborne bacteria.

## Pasteurization and Pathogen Control

The primary food safety concern with mayonnaise is **Salmonella enteritidis**, a bacterium commonly found inside raw eggs. 

To eliminate this hazard, commercial food manufacturers use **pasteurized liquid eggs**. The pasteurization process involves heating the eggs to a precise temperature (typically around **140°F / 60°C**) for a specific duration. This thermal process is high enough to kill any active Salmonella bacteria without cooking the egg proteins, allowing the eggs to emulsify properly into a smooth, creamy spread. 

Additionally, commercial mayonnaise is highly acidic due to the inclusion of vinegar and lemon juice, which creates an inhospitable environment for bacterial growth.

## Storage and Picnic Safety Cautions

While commercial mayonnaise is sterile when first opened, improper food handling can introduce bacteria:

*   **Refrigerate Immediately:** Once the vacuum seal is broken, store-bought mayonnaise must be kept in the refrigerator at or below **40°F (4°C)**.
*   **The 2-Hour Rule:** Never leave mayonnaise or mayonnaise-based foods (such as potato salad, chicken salad, or egg salad sandwiches) at room temperature for more than **2 hours** (or **1 hour** if the outdoor temperature is above 90°F / 32°C). Bacteria can grow rapidly on warm food, posing a risk of staphylococcal food poisoning.
*   **Picnics and Buffets:** At gatherings, keep all mayonnaise-containing salads nested in bowls of ice, or store them in a chilled cooler until right before serving.
*   **Prevent Cross-Contamination:** Always use a clean spoon or knife to scoop mayonnaise from the jar. Double-dipping with a utensil that has touched other foods can introduce bacteria into the jar, leading to mold or bacterial growth.
