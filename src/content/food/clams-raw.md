## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

**Raw clams** are high-risk foods that must be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

According to directives from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, consuming raw or undercooked shellfish poses a direct risk of severe foodborne illness to pregnant individuals and can lead to pregnancy loss or fetal injury.

Clams are filter feeders, pumping large quantities of ocean water through their bodies to filter out food particles. In doing so, they also accumulate and concentrate pathogens present in the water, such as agricultural runoff, sewage, or naturally occurring coastal bacteria. 

Eating them raw means ingesting these concentrated viruses and bacteria.

## Primary Pathogen Risks

Raw clams can transmit several highly dangerous infections:
1.  **Hepatitis A:** A highly contagious liver virus that can cause severe liver inflammation in the mother and increase the risk of preterm delivery or gestational complications.
2.  **Norovirus:** Triggers acute gastroenteritis, causing severe vomiting and diarrhea. This can lead to maternal dehydration and electrolyte imbalances, potentially inducing uterine contractions and preterm labor.
3.  **Vibrio vulnificus:** A bacterium found in marine environments. In pregnant individuals (who have altered immune systems), Vibrio can cause **septicemia** (blood poisoning), which is life-threatening for both mother and fetus.
4.  **E. coli and Salmonella:** Cause severe gastrointestinal illness and high fevers, which can trigger miscarriage or fetal distress.

## Safe Handling and Cooking Alternatives

Expectant mothers do not need to avoid clams entirely, provided they are cooked properly:
*   **Boil or Steam Thoroughly:** Cook clams until their shells open, then continue boiling them for an additional **4 to 9 minutes** to ensure the internal temperature reaches **145°F (63°C)**. Discard any clams that do not open during cooking.
*   **Safe Dishes:** Enjoy dishes like New England clam chowder, linguine with red or white clam sauce, or baked clams (clams casino), provided they are prepared under hygienic conditions.
