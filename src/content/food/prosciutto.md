## Safety Status: Avoid (Unless Heated)

Cold, uncooked prosciutto should be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

Because prosciutto is a dry-cured ham, it is prepared by rubbing raw pork legs with salt and aging them for several months. While the salting and drying processes reduce water activity to aid preservation, they do not involve heat. Consequently, health agencies like the **U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** classify uncooked prosciutto as raw meat, presenting significant food safety hazards for pregnant individuals.

However, prosciutto is completely **safe** to consume if it has been baked, fried, or grilled until **sizzling and steaming hot** right before consumption.

## Pathogens of Concern: Toxoplasmosis & Listeriosis

Eating uncooked prosciutto presents a dual risk of severe infections:

*   **Toxoplasmosis:** Caused by the parasite *Toxoplasma gondii*, which can reside in the muscle tissue of pigs. The traditional dry-curing process does not guarantee the destruction of these parasitic cysts. If a pregnant individual contracts Toxoplasmosis, the parasite can cross the placenta. This can result in miscarriage, stillbirth, or severe congenital anomalies in the baby, such as hearing loss, vision impairment, and cerebral calcifications.
*   **Listeriosis:** Sliced cured meats are highly susceptible to cross-contamination with the bacterium *Listeria monocytogenes* during slicing and packaging at the deli counter. Listeria can survive and grow in cold, salty environments. Listeria infection during pregnancy can lead to premature delivery or severe neonatal sepsis.

## Safe Reheating and Portion Guidelines

To enjoy prosciutto safely during pregnancy:

*   **Bake Until Crispy:** Bake prosciutto slices in the oven at **375°F (190°C)** or pan-fry them until they curl up, turn dark, and become crispy, similar to bacon. This high heat destroys all pathogens.
*   **Use in Hot Dishes:** Add prosciutto to dishes that undergo thorough cooking, such as baked pastas, hot paninis, or cooked pizzas. Ensure the dish is served piping hot.
*   **Limit Sodium Intake:** Prosciutto is exceptionally high in sodium due to the heavy salting process. A single ounce can contain over 500 mg of sodium. Excess sodium can worsen pregnancy swelling (**edema**) in the ankles and hands, and increase blood pressure. Enjoy cooked prosciutto in moderation.
*   **Sanitize Kitchen Utensils:** Wash hands and any surfaces that touch cold prosciutto immediately to prevent cross-contamination.
