## Safety Status: Safe (Aged Hard Cheese)

Traditional and commercial **Parmesan cheese** (including authentic Italian *Parmigiano-Reggiano*) is completely **safe** to eat during pregnancy. 

While pregnant individuals are advised by the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** to avoid soft, unpasteurized cheeses due to the risk of **Listeria monocytogenes**, Parmesan is a major exception. Even when made from raw (unpasteurized) milk, Parmesan is classified as a hard cheese, which carries an extremely low risk of bacterial contamination.

The reason for this safety lies in the cheese-making process. Parmesan is cooked at high temperatures and aged for at least **12 to 36 months**. During this long maturation period, the moisture content of the cheese decreases significantly, and the salt concentration rises. 

Because Listeria requires water to grow and reproduce, the extremely low water activity and high acidity of aged Parmesan cause any pathogens to die off naturally. Therefore, it does not support the survival of Listeria.

## Nutritional Benefits

Parmesan is an exceptionally nutrient-dense cheese, offering great benefits during pregnancy:
*   **High Calcium Content:** Parmesan is one of the richest dietary sources of calcium. Just one ounce provides over 300 mg of calcium, which is essential for the development of your baby’s skeletal system.
*   **Protein Source:** It provides a high concentration of complete protein, aiding in maternal tissue growth and fetal development.
*   **Low Lactose:** Due to the long aging process, most of the lactose is broken down, making it easier to digest for individuals with mild lactose sensitivity.

## Safe Handling and Storage Guidelines

Keep your Parmesan fresh and safe with these clean kitchen rules:
*   **Store in Dry Refrigeration:** Wrap block Parmesan in parchment paper or wax paper, then loosely in plastic wrap or foil. Keep it stored in the refrigerator below **40°F (4°C)**.
*   **Watch for Mold:** Although hard cheeses are low-risk, surface mold can occasionally grow if moisture gets into the packaging. If a hard block of Parmesan develops a small spot of mold, you can cut away the mold plus at least **1 inch** around and below it. The remaining cheese is safe to eat.
*   **Hygienic Grating:** Wash your cheese grater thoroughly with warm soap and water before and after grating to prevent cross-contamination.
