## Safety Status: Safe to Consume

Thoroughly cooked, well-done pork is **safe** and highly nutritious to consume during pregnancy. 

According to guidelines from the **U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, pork is safe to consume once it has been cooked to specific temperature targets. These temperatures are designed to destroy common meat-borne pathogens—including parasites like *Trichinella spiralis* and bacteria like *Salmonella* and *Escherichia coli (E. coli)*. 

Ensuring pork is fully cooked eliminates these risks, allowing you to safely enjoy its nutritional benefits.

## Fetal Growth and Nutritional Benefits of Pork

Pork is a nutrient-dense protein that provides several essential vitamins and minerals for pregnancy:

*   **Thiamine (Vitamin B1):** Pork is an outstanding source of thiamine, a B vitamin that plays a key role in fetal brain development and helps convert carbohydrates into energy.
*   **Niacin (Vitamin B3) and Vitamin B6:** These B vitamins support healthy maternal skin, digestive health, and nervous system function, and can help alleviate morning sickness symptoms.
*   **High-Quality Protein:** Essential for the rapid cellular growth and tissue development of your baby, as well as the placenta.
*   **Zinc and Iron:** Support immune system development, cell division, and healthy red blood cell production, helping prevent prenatal anemia.

## USDA Safe Temperature Standards for Pork

To ensure pork is completely free of pathogens, you must follow these specific USDA temperature targets:

*   **Pork Chops, Steaks, and Roasts:** Cook to a minimum internal temperature of **145°F (63°C)**, as measured at the thickest part of the meat with a food thermometer. After cooking, you must allow the pork to **rest for at least 3 minutes** before cutting or eating. The rest period allows the internal temperature to stabilize or continue rising, ensuring any remaining pathogens are destroyed.
*   **Ground Pork (Sausages, Pork Patties):** Ground pork must reach an internal temperature of **160°F (71°C)**. Grinding mixes surface bacteria throughout the meat, requiring a higher internal temperature to ensure safety. A rest period is not required for ground pork.
*   **Always Use a Thermometer:** Never judge pork safety by color. Ground pork can turn brown before it is fully cooked, and whole cuts can look pink even when they have reached 145°F and rested. Always verify with a digital thermometer.
