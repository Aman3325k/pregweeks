## Safety Status: Safe to Consume

Commercially pasteurized honey is entirely **safe** to consume during pregnancy. 

Unlike raw honey—which is unprocessed—pasteurized honey undergoes heat processing. The **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** confirm that pasteurized honey is a secure ingredient for maternal diets, offering a safe sweetener option during pregnancy.

## The Pasteurization Process and Pathogen Control

To make commercial honey, packers heat the liquid:

*   **Heat Treatment:** Honey is heated to approximately **160°F (71°C)**, followed by rapid cooling.
*   **Destruction of Yeasts:** The primary reason packers pasteurize honey is to destroy wild yeasts (*Saccharomyces*). These yeasts can ferment honey over time, especially if the moisture content is high.
*   **Preventing Crystallization:** Heating dissolves any sugar crystals present in the liquid, keeping the honey smooth, clear, and easy to pour for longer periods.
*   **Spores:** While pasteurization is highly effective at killing yeast and bacteria, it may not destroy all spores of *Clostridium botulinum*. However, as with raw honey, a pregnant individual's mature stomach acidity and gut microbiome easily destroy these spores, preventing any risk to the fetus.

## Health Benefits and Diet Cautions

Pasteurized honey serves several useful purposes in a prenatal diet:
*   **Natural Cough Suppressant:** Honey is a clinically proven natural demulcent. Mixing pasteurized honey with warm water, fresh lemon, or decaf chamomile tea can soothe a cough and coat an irritated throat. This is a helpful alternative since many over-the-counter cough syrups are restricted during pregnancy.
*   **Energy Source:** Provides a quick, easily digestible source of carbohydrates.

However, honey is a concentrated source of sugars (fructose and glucose):
*   **Gestational Diabetes:** If you have gestational diabetes, honey will cause a rapid spike in blood glucose levels. Use it sparingly and monitor your portion sizes.
*   **Infant Warning:** Although pasteurized honey is safe for you, **never feed honey of any kind to a baby under one year of age**. The baby’s immature digestive system cannot defend against botulism spores, which can lead to infant botulism.
