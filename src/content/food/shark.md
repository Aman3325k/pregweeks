## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

**Shark** is classified as a high-risk food that must be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

According to joint dietary guidelines from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)**, shark is placed on the "Choices to Avoid" list for pregnant individuals, breastfeeding mothers, and young children.

The reason for this warning is the presence of extremely high levels of **methylmercury**. Methylmercury is a heavy metal and neurotoxin that accumulates in aquatic ecosystems. 

Because sharks are long-lived predatory fish at the top of the food chain, they absorb mercury from all the fish they consume. This biological concentration (biomagnification) results in mercury levels in shark muscle tissues that are far higher than in smaller, shorter-lived species.

## The Impact of Methylmercury on Fetal Development

During gestation, the fetal brain and nervous system are growing rapidly and are highly vulnerable to neurotoxic substances. 

When a pregnant individual consumes shark, the methylmercury in the fish is absorbed into the bloodstream and easily crosses the placental barrier. 

Even moderate maternal consumption can result in elevated blood mercury levels in the fetus, which can disrupt brain architecture. Long-term studies show that prenatal mercury exposure is linked to:
*   Delayed cognitive development and lower intelligence scores.
*   Impaired language and motor coordination skills.
*   Deficits in attention span, visual-spatial processing, and memory.

## Safe Seafood Alternatives

Seafood is an excellent source of lean protein, zinc, iron, and omega-3 fatty acids (DHA and EPA), which are highly beneficial for baby development. Instead of high-mercury fish like shark, follow FDA guidelines and consume **8 to 12 ounces per week** of safe, low-mercury alternatives:
*   **Wild-Caught Salmon:** High in DHA omega-3s and extremely low in mercury.
*   **Cod or Haddock:** Mild white fish that are low in mercury.
*   **Canned Light Tuna:** Safest choice for tuna lovers (avoid Bigeye or Albacore in large amounts).
*   **Shrimp and Anchovies:** Low-mercury seafood options.
