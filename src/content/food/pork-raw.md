## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

Raw or undercooked pork (including raw pork chops, roasts, ribs, ground pork, and raw bacon) must be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

According to guidelines from the **U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, raw pork is a high-risk source of foodborne parasites and bacteria. Consuming undercooked pork or failing to practice proper hygiene when handling raw meat can lead to severe gastrointestinal infections and parasitic diseases, posing serious threats to maternal and fetal health.

## Parasites and Pathogens in Raw Pork

Raw pork can harbor several dangerous pathogens:

*   **Trichinella spiralis:** This roundworm parasite causes trichinosis. When raw or undercooked pork containing Trichinella larvae is consumed, the larvae mature and migrate into maternal muscles, causing fever, severe muscle pain, facial swelling, and weakness. While commercial farming has reduced the prevalence of this parasite, raw pork remains a risk.
*   **Toxoplasma gondii:** Pork is a common source of Toxoplasma tissue cysts. If a pregnant individual contracts Toxoplasmosis, the parasite can cross the placenta, leading to miscarriage, stillbirth, or severe congenital neurological and ocular damage in the baby.
*   **Salmonella and E. Coli:** Raw pork can be contaminated with Salmonella and E. coli bacteria during processing, causing severe food poisoning, high fever, and vomiting. Dehydration from these infections can trigger premature labor.

## Safe Handling and Preparation Guidelines

To prevent cross-contamination and ensure safety in your kitchen:

*   **Cook to Safe Temperatures:** 
    *   **Whole Cuts (Chops, Steaks, Roasts):** Cook to a minimum internal temperature of **145°F (63°C)**, and allow the meat to **rest for at least 3 minutes** before carving or serving.
    *   **Ground Pork (Sausages, Patties):** Cook to an internal temperature of **160°F (71°C)**. Ground meat mixes surface bacteria throughout the product, requiring a higher temperature.
*   **Use a Food Thermometer:** Always insert a digital food thermometer into the thickest part of the pork to verify the temperature. Do not rely on meat color.
*   **Practice Strict Handwashing:** Wash your hands with warm water and soap for at least 20 seconds immediately after touching raw pork or its packaging.
*   **Sanitize Prep Areas:** Wash cutting boards, knives, and countertops with hot, soapy water immediately after raw meat prep. Dedicate a separate cutting board only for raw meats.
