## Safety Status: Moderate (Limit Intake)

**Chamomile tea** is safe to consume during pregnancy, but only in **moderation**. 

Unlike peppermint or ginger tea (which are classified as safe), chamomile (*Matricaria recutita*) falls into a moderate safety category. 

While a weak, occasional cup of chamomile tea is generally considered safe, consuming large quantities or highly concentrated chamomile extracts can act as a **uterine stimulant**, potentially triggering contractions or labor.

Additionally, chamomile belongs to the Asteraceae (ragweed) family. 

If you have a known allergy to ragweed, daisies, marigolds, or chrysanthemums, you must avoid chamomile tea entirely, as it can trigger allergic reactions ranging from skin rashes to respiratory distress.

## Benefits and Cautions for Pregnancy

When consumed in small, weak amounts (no more than **one cup per day**), chamomile tea can offer helpful prenatal benefits:
*   **Insomnia Relief:** Chamomile contains an antioxidant called **apigenin**, which binds to specific receptors in your brain to promote sleepiness and reduce anxiety, helping manage pregnancy-related sleep issues.
*   **Digestive Comfort:** It can help soothe minor stomach irritation, bloating, and gas.

However, pregnant women must adhere to these cautions:
*   **Uterine Contractions:** High doses of chamomile have uterine-stimulating properties. It is best to avoid drinking it during the first trimester when the risk of miscarriage is highest.
*   **Anti-inflammatory Effects:** Chamomile has mild anti-inflammatory properties similar to NSAIDs. In late pregnancy (third trimester), extremely high consumption of anti-inflammatory herbs could theoretically affect the fetal *ductus arteriosus* (a key blood vessel in the baby's heart), though this risk is primarily associated with high-dose medications.

## Safe Preparation Tips

To enjoy chamomile tea safely:
*   **Brew it Weak:** Steep the tea bag for only **2 to 3 minutes** instead of the standard 5 to 10 minutes to keep the active compounds diluted.
*   **Limit Frequency:** Do not drink it daily; restrict usage to a few times a week or single cups on nights when you struggle to sleep.
*   **Avoid Extracts:** Do not use chamomile tinctures, concentrated oils, or herbal supplements, as these contain dangerous concentrations of the plant's active compounds.
