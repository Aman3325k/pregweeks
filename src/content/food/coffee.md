## Safety Status: In Moderation

Coffee is safe to drink during pregnancy, but it must be consumed **in moderation**. The active compound of concern is **caffeine**, a central nervous system stimulant. 

Caffeine easily crosses the placenta and enters the baby's bloodstream. While your body has enzymes to metabolize caffeine, a developing fetus has immature organ systems and cannot process caffeine efficiently. High levels of caffeine in the fetal bloodstream can constrict uterine blood vessels, reducing blood flow to the placenta and potentially restricting fetal growth.

The consensus guideline from the **American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG)**, the World Health Organization (WHO), and the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) states that **pregnant women should limit caffeine intake to less than 200 mg per day**. This is roughly equivalent to one 12-ounce cup of standard brewed coffee. 

## Nutritional Profile & Considerations

Coffee itself does not provide significant essential nutrients for pregnancy, although it contains antioxidants. In fact, drinking coffee can slightly interfere with your body's ability to absorb non-heme iron (the iron found in plant foods and prenatal vitamins) if consumed during or immediately after meals. Since iron is essential for producing red blood cells and preventing gestational anemia, it is best to drink coffee at least one hour before or after meals or vitamins.

## Safe Preparation & Consuming Guidelines

To stay safely under the 200 mg daily limit, it is important to understand how much caffeine is in your favorite drinks:
*   **Drip Brewed Coffee (8 oz):** Contains 95–140 mg of caffeine.
*   **Espresso (1 shot / 1 oz):** Contains 63–75 mg of caffeine. (Note: A double shot espresso drink contains 130–150 mg).
*   **Cold Brew Coffee (12 oz):** Often contains higher amounts (150–200+ mg) because of the prolonged steeping process.
*   **Decaf Coffee (8 oz):** Contains only 2–5 mg, making it a highly safe choice.

## Healthy Alternatives

If you want to reduce your caffeine intake further, try these excellent alternatives:
*   **Swiss Water Decaf:** Made using a natural water process that avoids chemical solvent residues.
*   **Chicory Root Coffee:** A caffeine-free herbal drink that tastes remarkably similar to dark roast coffee.
*   **Rooibos Tea:** A naturally caffeine-free herbal tea loaded with antioxidants.
*   **Lemon Water:** Warm water with fresh lemon can help settle an upset stomach and support hydration.
