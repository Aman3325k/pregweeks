## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

Traditional homemade eggnog is classified under the **avoid** category during pregnancy. 

According to guidelines from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, homemade eggnog made with raw eggs is a primary source of **Salmonella enteritidis** infections. Salmonella is a bacterium that can be present inside raw eggs, even if the shell is clean and uncracked. 

Salmonellosis (Salmonella food poisoning) causes severe vomiting, diarrhea, high fever, and abdominal cramps. In pregnant individuals, severe dehydration and high fever can lead to pregnancy complications, including uterine irritability, cramping, or preterm labor.

Furthermore, traditional homemade eggnog often contains alcohol (such as rum, brandy, or bourbon). Alcohol must be avoided entirely during pregnancy as it crosses the placenta, presenting severe risks of neurodevelopmental issues and Fetal Alcohol Syndrome.

## Safe Alternatives & Cooking Rules

You can safely enjoy eggnog during the holiday season with these options:
*   **Commercially Pasteurized Eggnog:** Purchase store-bought eggnog from the dairy section of your supermarket. Commercial eggnog is pasteurized, which involves heat-treating the beverage to kill all harmful bacteria, and is non-alcoholic.
*   **Make a Cooked Custard Base:** If making eggnog at home, use a recipe that requires cooking the egg and milk mixture. Heat the liquid slowly until it reaches an internal temperature of **160°F (71°C)** or coats the back of a spoon. This temperature destroys Salmonella, making the mixture safe.
*   **Use Pasteurized Liquid Eggs:** Substitute regular raw eggs with commercially pasteurized liquid eggs in your recipe.

## Kitchen Hygiene & Storage

When preparing cooked eggnog:
*   **Use a Food Thermometer:** Do not guess the temperature of your eggnog base. Use a digital food thermometer to confirm it has reached 160°F (71°C).
*   **Wash Hands and Utensils:** Wash your hands, bowls, and whisks thoroughly with warm soapy water after handling raw eggs.
*   **Refrigerate Promptly:** Keep pasteurized eggnog refrigerated at or below **40°F (4°C)** and consume it within 3 days of preparation.
