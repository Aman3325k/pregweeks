## Safety Status: Moderate (Limit Caffeine Intake)

**Espresso** is safe to consume during pregnancy, but only in **moderation**. 

The **American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG)**, along with the **World Health Organization (WHO)** and the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)**, recommends that pregnant individuals limit their daily caffeine consumption to **less than 200 mg per day**.

A single shot of espresso (approximately 1 fluid ounce) contains between **63 and 75 mg of caffeine**, depending on the bean type (Robusta beans contain more caffeine than Arabica) and the brewing method. 

A standard double-shot espresso drink (like a latte or cappuccino) will therefore contain **130 to 150 mg of caffeine**, which constitutes about 75% of your recommended daily limit.

## Biological Effects of Caffeine on the Fetus

It is important to manage caffeine intake because of how it behaves in the body during pregnancy:
*   **Placental Transfer:** Caffeine easily crosses the placental barrier and enters the baby's bloodstream. While your body can metabolize caffeine, a developing fetus lacks the necessary enzymes to process it efficiently, meaning caffeine can remain in the fetal system longer.
*   **Vasoconstriction:** Caffeine is a stimulant that can cause mild constriction of blood vessels in the placenta, potentially reducing blood flow and nutrient delivery to the fetus.
*   **Fetal Heart Rate:** High caffeine doses can cause temporary increases in fetal heart rate and activity levels.
*   **Hydration and Sleep:** Caffeine is a diuretic that can contribute to dehydration, and it can worsen pregnancy insomnia or acid reflux.

## Tips for Managing Caffeine Consumption

To enjoy espresso safely while staying below the 200 mg threshold:
*   **Track All Sources:** Remember that caffeine is found in many foods and drinks, including drip coffee, black and green tea, colas, energy drinks, chocolate, and certain over-the-counter headache medications.
*   **Switch to Decaf:** Decaffeinated espresso is made from beans that have had 97% of their caffeine removed, containing only about **2 to 5 mg of caffeine** per shot, making it an excellent safe alternative.
*   **Specify Single Shots:** When ordering a latte, flat white, or cappuccino at a coffee shop, explicitly ask for a "single shot" of espresso, as many establishments default to double shots.
