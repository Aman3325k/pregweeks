## Safety Status: Consume in Strict Moderation (Or Avoid)

Animal liver (such as beef liver, chicken liver, pork liver, or liver sausage) should be consumed in **strict moderation** or **avoided entirely** during pregnancy. 

While liver is an exceptional source of iron, protein, and other essential nutrients, its concentration of **preformed Vitamin A (retinol)** is extremely high. Medical organizations, including the **American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG)** and the **World Health Organization (WHO)**, recommend that pregnant individuals restrict their consumption of liver and liver products to prevent the risk of Vitamin A toxicity.

## Preformed Vitamin A (Retinol) and Birth Defects

Vitamin A is essential for fetal development, supporting vision, immune function, and bone growth. However, it exists in two distinct forms:

*   **Preformed Vitamin A (Retinol):** Found in animal-derived foods, especially liver. Retinol is fat-soluble and easily stored in the maternal liver. Because it accumulates in the body and crosses the placenta, high doses are toxic to a developing fetus. Excessive intake of retinol (exceeding **10,000 IU or 3,000 mcg RAE** per day) is linked to congenital anomalies. Fetal risks include cranial neural crest defects, congenital heart defects, and central nervous system abnormalities.
*   **Provitamin A (Beta-Carotene):** Found in yellow, orange, and leafy green vegetables (like sweet potatoes, carrots, and spinach). The maternal body only converts beta-carotene into Vitamin A as needed. Beta-carotene is not toxic and does not pose a risk of birth defects, making plant-based Vitamin A safe.

## Portions and Prenatal Iron Alternatives

If you choose to eat liver during pregnancy, you must strictly control your portion sizes:

*   **Limit Portions:** Restrict intake to no more than **1 to 2 ounces (30 to 60 grams) once a week**. Eating a standard portion of liver (3 ounces) can contain over 20,000 IU of retinol, which is twice the daily upper limit.
*   **Avoid Liver in the First Trimester:** Fetal organs are forming during the first trimester, making the fetus highly vulnerable to teratogens. Avoid liver completely during this critical period.
*   **Alternative Iron Sources:** Since iron is vital to prevent gestational anemia, look to safer dietary options. Choose lean beef or chicken cooked to safe temperatures, beans, lentils, pumpkin seeds, spinach, and your doctor-recommended prenatal vitamins.
