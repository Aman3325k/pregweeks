## Safety Status: Moderate (Limit Caffeine Intake)

**Black tea** is **safe** to consume during pregnancy, but only in **moderation**. 

Because black tea is derived from the *Camellia sinensis* plant, it naturally contains caffeine. The **American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG)** recommends that pregnant individuals limit their total caffeine consumption to **less than 200 mg per day** from all sources. 

A standard 8-ounce cup of brewed black tea contains approximately **40 to 70 mg of caffeine**. This means drinking 2 to 3 cups of black tea could bring you close to or over your daily safety limit, especially if you also consume chocolate, coffee, or sodas.

## Tannins and Nutrient Absorption

Beyond caffeine, black tea contains high levels of **tannins** and **polyphenols**. While these are beneficial antioxidants for non-pregnant adults, they can interfere with nutrient absorption during pregnancy:
*   **Iron Absorption:** Tannins bind to non-heme iron—the type of iron found in plants, fortified grains, and prenatal vitamins. This binding makes it harder for your body to absorb iron. Because pregnant women need extra iron to support a 50% expansion in blood volume and prevent anemia, it is best to drink black tea at least **1 hour before or after** eating meals or taking prenatal vitamins.
*   **Folic Acid:** Excessive tea consumption has also been linked in some studies to slightly reduced absorption of folic acid, which is critical in the first trimester to prevent neural tube defects.

## Safe Preparation Tips

To enjoy black tea safely:
*   **Reduce Brew Time:** Steep your tea bag for **1 to 2 minutes** instead of the typical 5 minutes. This releases the flavor while reducing caffeine and tannin concentrations.
*   **Switch to Decaf:** Decaffeinated black tea is a safe alternative. Ensure it is decaffeinated using a safe process, such as the carbon dioxide or water process, which avoids chemical solvents like ethyl acetate.
*   **Monitor Additives:** If you like milk and sugar in your tea, choose pasteurized milk and limit added sugars, especially if you are managing gestational diabetes.
