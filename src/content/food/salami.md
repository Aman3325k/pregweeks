## Safety Status: Avoid (Unless Heated)

Cold, uncooked salami should be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

Like other cured meats, salami is made by mixing ground meat (often pork or beef) with salt, spices, and yeast or starter cultures, allowing it to ferment, and then air-drying it. The acidity produced during fermentation and the drying process helps preserve the meat, but the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** and the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** emphasize that curing does not kill all bacteria or parasites. 

Salami is only safe to consume during pregnancy if it is heated until **steaming hot** (reaching an internal temperature of **165°F / 74°C**) right before serving.

## Fetal Risks: Listeria & Toxoplasmosis

Undercooked or cold cured salami presents several serious safety concerns for the developing fetus:

*   **Toxoplasmosis:** The parasite *Toxoplasma gondii* is a major risk in uncooked cured pork and beef. Fermentation and curing are not sufficient to reliably destroy the parasite's tissue cysts. If a pregnant individual contracts Toxoplasmosis, it can cross the placental barrier and cause severe birth defects, including cerebral calcifications, hydrocephalus, hearing loss, and congenital blindness.
*   **Listeriosis:** Salami can become contaminated with *Listeria monocytogenes* during the slicing and packaging processes. Unlike most bacteria, Listeria can grow at refrigerator temperatures and survive in low-moisture, high-salt foods. Listeriosis can trigger miscarriage, stillbirth, preterm labor, or severe illness in the newborn.
*   **Salmonella:** Can survive the curing process if contaminated raw meat is used, causing severe gastrointestinal distress and dehydration.

## Safe Preparation & Portion Guidelines

To safely include salami in your pregnancy diet:

*   **Heat Until Steaming Hot:** Reheat salami slices in a skillet, oven, or microwave until they are piping hot (at least **165°F / 74°C**) and emitting visible steam. If microwaving, place the slices on a paper towel and microwave on high for 15 to 20 seconds.
*   **Bake in Dishes:** Salami is safe when baked into hot dishes like paninis, pizzas, calzones, or pasta bakes, provided the entire dish is cooked through and served hot.
*   **Avoid Deli Platters:** Strictly avoid cold deli platters, charcuterie boards, or pre-made cold sandwiches containing salami, as these are high-risk sources of cross-contamination.
*   **Monitor Sodium and Preservatives:** Salami is extremely high in sodium and saturated fats, and is cured with sodium nitrites/nitrates. High sodium intake can worsen pregnancy swelling (edema) and raise blood pressure, so consume heated salami in small quantities as an occasional treat.
