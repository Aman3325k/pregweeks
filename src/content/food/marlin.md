## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

**Marlin** is a high-risk seafood option that must be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

The **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)** classify Marlin on their list of "Choices to Avoid" for pregnant individuals, women who are breastfeeding, and young children.

Marlins are large, migratory billfish that inhabit tropical and temperate ocean waters. As apex predators, they consume large quantities of smaller fish and squid. 

Because mercury cannot be easily excreted by marine life, it accumulates in the food chain. When a marlin eats smaller fish, it absorbs all the mercury previously accumulated by those prey animals. Over the marlin's long lifespan, this process (biomagnification) results in high mercury concentrations.

## Neurological Risks to the Fetus

When a pregnant woman eats marlin, the methylmercury in the fish is absorbed into her bloodstream and crosses the placenta, directly reaching the developing fetus. 

Because the fetal nervous system is growing at an extraordinary rate, it is highly sensitive to the toxic effects of heavy metals. 

High prenatal exposure to mercury can lead to permanent damage, including:
*   **Developmental Delays:** Delayed milestones in sitting, walking, and talking.
*   **Cognitive Deficits:** Lower learning capability and visual-spatial deficits.
*   **Motor Impairment:** Coordination issues, muscle weakness, and tremors.
*   **Behavioral Challenges:** Problems with attention span and concentration.

## Healthy, Low-Mercury Alternatives

You should continue to eat fish during pregnancy, as it contains essential nutrients like lean protein, iron, and DHA omega-3 fatty acids, which are crucial for fetal eye and brain development. 

The FDA recommends eating **8 to 12 ounces (2 to 3 servings)** of low-mercury seafood per week, such as:
*   **Rainbow Trout:** High in omega-3s and extremely low in mercury.
*   **Salmon:** An excellent, safe source of prenatal DHA.
*   **Pacific Halibut:** Safe to eat in moderation (limit to one serving per week).
*   **Cod or Haddock:** Safe, low-mercury white fish choices.
