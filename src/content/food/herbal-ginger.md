## Safety Status: Safe (Caffeine-Free)

**Ginger tea** is **safe** to consume during pregnancy and is highly recommended by obstetricians, midwives, and clinical organizations, including the **American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG)**, as a first-line remedy for pregnancy-induced nausea.

Because ginger tea is a herbal infusion, it is naturally caffeine-free and does not interfere with your daily prenatal caffeine limits. 

The active compounds in ginger, specifically **gingerols** and **shogaols**, act locally in the digestive system to accelerate stomach emptying and block receptors in the gut that trigger vomiting.

## Clinical Benefits of Ginger Tea

Ginger tea offers several health benefits for pregnant individuals:
*   **Morning Sickness Relief:** Numerous clinical trials demonstrate that ginger is highly effective at reducing the severity of nausea and vomiting during the first trimester.
*   **Heartburn and Indigestion Relief:** Unlike peppermint (which relaxes the esophageal sphincter and can trigger acid reflux), ginger helps reduce stomach acid and promotes forward digestion, making it excellent for managing pregnancy-related heartburn.
*   **Anti-Inflammatory Properties:** Can help alleviate minor muscle aches and joint pain associated with pregnancy weight changes.

## Dosage and Safety Limits

While ginger is highly safe, moderation is key:
*   **Daily Limit:** Clinical safety guidelines advise pregnant women not to exceed **1,000 mg (1 gram)** of dried ginger extract (or raw ginger equivalent) per day. Consuming excessively high doses (such as those found in concentrated ginger supplements or pills) can theoretically increase the risk of bleeding due to mild anti-platelet effects.
*   **Brewing Guidelines:** 1 to 2 cups of brewed ginger tea per day is completely safe. To brew, steep a few slices of fresh ginger root in boiling water for 5 to 10 minutes.
*   **Check Tea Blends:** If buying pre-packaged tea bags, ensure the box contains 100% ginger root or is mixed only with safe ingredients (like lemon or peppermint), rather than unsafe fillers.
