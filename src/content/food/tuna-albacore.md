## Safety Status: In Moderation

Albacore (often sold as "white" tuna, whether canned, pouched, or fresh) is classified under the **moderate** safety category during pregnancy. 

While it is an excellent source of protein and Omega-3 fatty acids, it contains moderate levels of **methylmercury**. Methylmercury is a heavy metal that bioaccumulates in fish tissue over time. 

The FDA and EPA guidelines recommend that pregnant individuals limit their consumption of Albacore tuna to **no more than 6 ounces (one average serving) per week**. Consuming amounts beyond this threshold increases the risk of mercury accumulation in the maternal bloodstream, which can cross the placenta and potentially affect the baby's developing brain and nervous system.

## Understanding Mercury Levels: Albacore vs. Light Tuna

When shopping for tuna, it is critical to understand the distinction between varieties:
*   **Albacore (White) Tuna:** Made from larger Albacore fish. Because they are higher up the food chain and live longer, their tissues contain significantly more mercury.
*   **Canned Light Tuna:** Typically made from **skipjack** or **tongol** tuna. These are smaller fish with shorter lifespans, resulting in much lower mercury levels. Pregnant individuals can safely eat up to 12 ounces (2 to 3 servings) of canned light tuna per week.
*   **Bigeye Tuna:** A large species that contains exceptionally high mercury levels. It is on the FDA's "Avoid" list and should not be eaten at all during pregnancy.

## Safe Consumption Guidelines & Alternatives

If you decide to include Albacore tuna in your prenatal diet:
*   **Track Servings:** Limit consumption to one 6-ounce serving weekly and do not consume any other fish that week.
*   **Swap for Light Tuna:** Choose canned light tuna in water instead of oil to keep calories moderate while doubling your safe weekly seafood allowance.
*   **Choose High-Omega, Low-Mercury Alternatives:** If you want to increase your intake of brain-boosting DHA without mercury concerns, swap tuna for wild-caught salmon, sardines, or rainbow trout.
