## Safety Status: Safe (Fully Cooked)

Fully cooked **lobster** is **safe** and highly nutritious to consume during pregnancy. 

According to guidelines from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, lobster is classified as a "Good Choice" because it has low methylmercury levels, falling well below the safety thresholds set for expectant mothers.

However, you must strictly avoid **raw or undercooked lobster**. Raw shellfish can harbor harmful pathogens, including **Vibrio bacteria**, **Salmonella**, and **Listeria monocytogenes**. 

Listeriosis, in particular, is a severe foodborne illness that can cause miscarriage, stillbirth, or severe infections in newborns. Cooking lobster to the correct temperature destroys these biological hazards, making it safe to eat.

## Nutritional Benefits

Lobster is a nutrient-dense food that supports a healthy pregnancy:
*   **High-Quality Protein:** Essential for the cellular growth and development of your baby.
*   **Zinc:** Supports cellular division, immune function, and DNA synthesis.
*   **Selenium:** Helps regulate maternal thyroid function and protects cells from oxidative damage.
*   **Omega-3 Fatty Acids:** Contains modest amounts of EPA and DHA, which aid in fetal brain and eye development.

## Safe Preparation and Cooking Guidelines

To enjoy lobster safely, follow these handling and cooking rules:
*   **Cook Thoroughly:** Boil, steam, or bake lobster until the internal temperature reaches **145°F (63°C)**. The shell should turn a bright red, and the meat should become firm and pearly white, not translucent.
*   **Avoid the Tomalley:** The tomalley is the green liver/pancreas found inside the lobster. Because the liver filters toxins, the tomalley can concentrate heavy metals and dangerous algae toxins (such as paralytic shellfish poison). Discard the tomalley entirely.
*   **Ensure Freshness:** Cook live lobsters immediately. If buying frozen lobster tails, thaw them in the refrigerator, not on the counter, and cook them immediately after thawing.
*   **Limit Portions:** As a "Good Choice" seafood, you can safely enjoy cooked lobster in amounts up to **one serving (6 ounces) per week**, as part of your total 8 to 12 ounces of weekly low-mercury seafood.
