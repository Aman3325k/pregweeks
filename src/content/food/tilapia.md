## Safety Status: Safe (Fully Cooked)

Fully cooked **tilapia** is completely **safe** to eat during pregnancy. 

Under the joint guidelines of the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)**, tilapia is featured on the "Best Choices" list. It consistently shows some of the lowest levels of methylmercury among commercially available fish.

As with all seafood, you must avoid **raw or undercooked tilapia**. Raw fish can contain harmful foodborne pathogens, including **Salmonella**, **Escherichia coli (E. coli)**, and **Listeria monocytogenes**. 

Cooking tilapia to the correct internal temperature kills these microorganisms, ensuring a safe meal for you and your baby.

## Nutritional Value of Tilapia

Tilapia is a versatile, mild-flavored fish that offers several prenatal benefits:
*   **Lean Protein:** A single fillet provides about 23 grams of protein, which is essential for fetal cellular growth and supporting the mother's increased blood supply.
*   **Essential Minerals:** Contains selenium, phosphorus, and potassium, which support immune function and fluid balance.
*   **Low Fat:** It is a low-calorie, low-fat source of protein, making it easy to digest for mothers experiencing pregnancy heartburn or nausea.

## Safe Cooking and Sourcing Guidelines

Practice these safety habits when purchasing and cooking tilapia:
*   **Cook to 145°F (63°C):** Cook the fish until the thickest part reaches an internal temperature of 145°F. The flesh should be opaque and flake easily when tested with a fork.
*   **Source Responsibly:** Choose tilapia sourced from countries with strict aquaculture regulations (such as the United States, Canada, Ecuador, or Peru) to ensure the fish were raised in clean, monitored water systems.
*   **Portion Guide:** You can safely eat **8 to 12 ounces (2 to 3 servings)** of low-mercury fish per week, including cooked tilapia.
