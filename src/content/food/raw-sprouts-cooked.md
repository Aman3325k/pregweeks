## Safety Status: Safe to Consume

Thoroughly cooked sprouts (including alfalfa, clover, mung bean, and radish sprouts) are **safe** to consume during pregnancy. 

While raw sprouts are highly dangerous due to their potential to harbor *Salmonella* and *E. coli* within their seed cracks, heat treatment changes their safety profile. Bringing sprouts to a high temperature destroys these pathogens, making them safe for consumption.

The **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** state that cooking is the only reliable way to eliminate bacteria from sprouts.

## Pathogen Elimination via Heat

Sprouts grow from seeds in warm, humid conditions that are ideal for bacterial replication. 

*   **Bacteria in Seed Cracks:** Pathogens can enter tiny cracks in the seeds before the sprout grows, making washing ineffective.
*   **Heat Destroys Pathogens:** Fortunately, bacteria like *Salmonella* and *Escherichia coli* are sensitive to heat. Cooking sprouts thoroughly—so they reach an internal temperature of at least **165°F (74°C)** or are cooked until steaming hot throughout—kills these pathogens.
*   **Avoid Light Steaming:** Lightly steaming or tossing raw sprouts into a warm dish right before serving is unsafe. The heat must penetrate the seed portion of the sprout to ensure safety.

## Safe Preparation & Cooking Methods

To prepare sprouts safely at home:

*   **Sauté or Stir-Fry:** Cook sprouts in a hot pan or wok with a little oil. Sauté them for several minutes until they are limp, thoroughly cooked, and hot throughout. They should emit steam.
*   **Boil in Soups:** Add sprouts to boiling soups, stews, or broths (such as Vietnamese Pho or ramen). Ensure the soup remains at a rolling boil for several minutes after adding the sprouts.
*   **Bake in Casseroles:** Mix sprouts into casseroles or baked dishes that undergo long baking times.
*   **Inspect Before Prep:** Even if you plan to cook them, discard any sprouts that look slimy, smell musty, or have turned brown, as these are signs of spoilage and high bacterial load.
