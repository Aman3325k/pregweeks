## Safety Status: Safe (Fully Cooked)

Fully cooked **oysters** are **safe** and highly beneficial to eat during pregnancy. 

While raw oysters are strictly off-limits due to the high risk of foodborne illnesses from **Vibrio bacteria** and **Norovirus**, applying sufficient heat destroys these pathogens. 

The **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** state that cooking shellfish to an internal temperature of **145°F (63°C)** renders it completely safe for pregnant individuals.

## Nutritional Benefits of Cooked Oysters

Oysters are considered a nutritional powerhouse for prenatal health, offering essential minerals that are often difficult to get in sufficient quantities:
*   **Rich in Zinc:** Zinc is vital for fetal growth, supporting cellular division, tissue repair, and the development of the brain and immune system. Oysters contain more zinc per serving than any other food.
*   **High in Iron:** Iron is essential for the production of hemoglobin, helping prevent maternal anemia and supporting the expanded blood volume needed during pregnancy.
*   **Omega-3 Fatty Acids (DHA/EPA):** Supports the neurological and visual development of the fetus.
*   **Vitamin B12:** Crucial for nervous system development and preventing birth defects.

## Safe Cooking and Preparation Guidelines

To ensure oysters are cooked safely and thoroughly, follow these guidelines:
*   **In-Shell Cooking:** When boiling or steaming whole oysters, cook until the shells open. Continue cooking for another **3 to 5 minutes** to ensure the interior reaches safety temperatures. Discard any oysters that do not open during the cooking process.
*   **Shucked Oysters:** Boil shucked oysters for at least **3 minutes**, fry them in oil at **375°F (190°C)** for at least **3 minutes**, or bake them at **450°F (230°C)** for **10 minutes**.
*   **Check the Texture:** Cooked oysters should be firm and plump. Avoid eating any oysters that look shriveled, thin, or feel mushy.
*   **Limit Portions:** Oysters are low in mercury. You can safely consume up to **2 servings (8 to 12 ounces)** of cooked oysters per week as part of your healthy pregnancy diet.
