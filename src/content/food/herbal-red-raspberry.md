## Safety Status: Moderate (Avoid Until late Third Trimester)

**Red raspberry leaf tea** is safe to consume during pregnancy, but only **starting in the late third trimester** (typically around **Week 32 to Week 34**). It must be strictly avoided during the first and second trimesters.

Red raspberry leaf (*Rubus idaeus*) has a long history of traditional use as a uterine tonic. 

The leaves contain an organic compound called **fragarine**, which has been shown to cause the smooth muscles of the pelvis and uterus to contract or relax. 

In early pregnancy, when the uterus is highly sensitive and the placenta is still developing, stimulating these muscles can cause cramping and increase the risk of **uterine instability or miscarriage**.

## Benefits in the Third Trimester

Once you reach the late third trimester, the safety profile shifts under clinical supervision:
*   **Toning the Uterus:** Fragarine helps strengthen and tone the uterine wall. When labor eventually begins, a toned uterus can contract more efficiently.
*   **Labor Efficiency:** Some clinical research indicates that pregnant individuals who consumed red raspberry leaf tea in the final weeks of pregnancy experienced a shorter active pushing phase and a lower rate of artificial rupture of membranes, forceps delivery, or cesarean section.
*   **Postpartum Recovery:** The tea can help the uterus contract back to its pre-pregnancy size (involution) after delivery and help reduce postpartum bleeding.

## Safe Usage Guidelines

To use red raspberry leaf tea safely:
*   **Wait Until Week 32 or 34:** Do not begin drinking the tea until you are in the final weeks of your third trimester.
*   **Consult Your OB-GYN or Midwife:** Always ask your healthcare provider before starting the tea, especially if you have a history of premature labor, multiple gestations (twins), gestational diabetes, or high blood pressure.
*   **Gradual Introduction:** Start with one weak cup per day. If well-tolerated, you can gradually increase to a maximum of **2 cups per day**. If you experience strong Braxton Hicks contractions or cramping after drinking it, stop immediately.
*   **Avoid if VBAC Planning:** Some providers recommend avoiding uterine tonics if you are planning a Vaginal Birth After Cesarean (VBAC) due to uterine scarring considerations.
