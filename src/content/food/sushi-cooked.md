## Safety Status: Safe (Cooked/Vegetarian)

Sushi rolls made with fully cooked seafood or vegetables are **safe** to consume during pregnancy. 

While the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)** advise pregnant individuals to avoid raw fish sushi due to the risk of parasites (like *Anisakis*) and bacteria (like *Listeria* and *Salmonella*), fully cooking the seafood eliminates these biological hazards.

Common safe sushi options include:
*   **California Rolls:** Made with cooked imitation crab (surimi), cucumber, and avocado.
*   **Eel (Unagi) Rolls:** Made with fully cooked, grilled freshwater eel.
*   **Shrimp Tempura Rolls:** Deep-fried shrimp wrapped in rice and seaweed.
*   **Vegetarian Rolls:** Made with avocado, cucumber, sweet potato tempura, or pickled radish.

The primary concern when eating cooked sushi in a restaurant is **cross-contamination**. If the kitchen prep area, knives, cutting boards, or the chef's hands touch raw fish before preparing your cooked roll, pathogens can be transferred to your food.

## Low-Mercury Selection

Even when seafood is cooked, pregnant individuals must limit exposure to **methylmercury**, a heavy metal that can harm a developing baby’s nervous system. 
*   **Choose Low-Mercury cooked options:** Shrimp, crab, eel, salmon, and scallops are low in mercury and safe.
*   **Limit or Avoid High-Mercury options:** Avoid cooked tuna rolls (such as spicy tuna or tuna salad rolls if made with high-mercury tuna varieties like bigeye or yellowfin) and ensure you stay within the FDA's general seafood guidelines of **8 to 12 ounces** of low-mercury fish per week.

## Safe Restaurant Practices

To enjoy cooked sushi safely, follow these guidelines:
*   **Communicate Your Pregnancy:** Inform your server and the sushi chef that you are pregnant. Politely request that your rolls be prepared on a clean surface with clean hands, clean knives, and clean rolling mats.
*   **Ensure Proper Cooking:** Confirm that warm ingredients (like tempura or eel) are served piping hot.
*   **Eat Promptly:** Consume sushi immediately or refrigerate it within 2 hours. Do not eat sushi that has been sitting at room temperature.
