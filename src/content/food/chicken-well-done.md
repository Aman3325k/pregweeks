## Safety Status: Safe to Consume

Thoroughly cooked, well-done chicken (and other poultry like turkey and duck) is entirely **safe** and serves as an excellent source of protein during pregnancy. 

According to safety guidelines from the **U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, poultry must be cooked to a minimum internal temperature of **165°F (74°C)**. Reaching this temperature ensures that common foodborne bacteria such as *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter* are completely destroyed, eliminating the risk of foodborne infection.

## Fetal Development and Nutritional Benefits

Chicken is a lean, nutrient-dense protein that provides vital building blocks for your pregnancy:

*   **High-Quality Protein:** Protein is essential for the rapid growth of fetal tissues, the expansion of maternal blood volume, and the development of the placenta and uterine tissues.
*   **Vitamin B6:** Supports cognitive development and can help ease pregnancy-related morning sickness.
*   **Choline:** Essential for fetal brain development and neural tube formation.
*   **Heme Iron and Zinc:** Crucial for oxygen transport in the blood and supporting your baby's developing immune system.

## Safe Preparation and Temperature Guidelines

To prepare chicken safely, follow these essential practices:

*   **Cook to 165°F (74°C):** Insert a digital food thermometer into the thickest part of the chicken, ensuring it does not touch the bone (which heats faster and can give a false reading). For ground poultry, check the temperature in multiple spots. The meat must reach 165°F (74°C) before consumption.
*   **Do Not Wash Raw Chicken:** A common kitchen mistake is rinsing raw chicken in the sink. The CDC warns that washing raw poultry spreads bacteria up to 3 feet away through splashing water droplets. This contaminates your sink, faucet, counters, and nearby food items. Cooking is the only way to kill bacteria.
*   **Thawing Safely:** Never thaw raw chicken on the kitchen counter at room temperature, as this allows bacteria to multiply rapidly. Thaw chicken safely in the refrigerator, in cold water (changing the water every 30 minutes), or in the microwave if cooking immediately.

## Cross-Contamination Prevention

*   **Use Separate Boards:** Dedicate a specific cutting board only for raw meat and poultry, and use a separate board for vegetables and ready-to-eat foods.
*   **Sanitize Utensils:** Wash all knives, cutting boards, and dishes that have touched raw chicken with hot, soapy water immediately. Use a bleach solution or run them through a hot dishwasher cycle for extra sanitation.
*   **Wash Hands:** Wash your hands with warm water and soap for at least 20 seconds after handling raw poultry.
