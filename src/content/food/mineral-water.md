## Safety Status: Safe to Consume

Commercially bottled mineral water is **safe** to consume during pregnancy. Unlike spring water or tap water, true mineral water comes from protected underground geologically physical sources and must contain a constant level of dissolved minerals and trace elements at the point of bottling. 

Because bottled mineral water is regulated by food safety organizations like the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)**, it undergoes extensive filtration and disinfection processes to ensure it is completely free of harmful pathogens. However, you should avoid drinking untreated water directly from natural springs or wild mineral wells, as raw water can be contaminated with parasites (such as *Giardia* or *Cryptosporidium*) and pathogenic bacteria.

## Mineral Composition & Prenatal Benefits

Drinking mineral water can supplement your daily nutrient intake, providing several key minerals that support prenatal health:

*   **Calcium:** Essential for the development of your baby’s skeletal system, teeth, heart, nerves, and muscles. If you do not consume enough calcium, your body will draw it from your own bones to support the fetus.
*   **Magnesium:** Helps regulate blood pressure, controls blood glucose levels, supports protein synthesis, and may reduce the frequency of pregnancy-related leg cramps.
*   **Bicarbonates:** Can help neutralize stomach acid, offering mild relief from pregnancy heartburn and indigestion.

## Key Considerations and Safety Guidelines

While mineral water is highly beneficial, there are a few important considerations for pregnant individuals:

*   **Sodium Content:** Some mineral water brands naturally contain high levels of sodium (exceeding 200 mg per liter). High sodium intake can worsen pregnancy bloating, edema (swelling of the hands and feet), and contribute to gestational hypertension. Read nutrition labels and choose brands with low sodium content (less than 20–50 mg per liter).
*   **Carbonation and Digestion:** Sparkling mineral water is carbonated. While carbonation is safe for the fetus, it can cause or worsen bloating, flatulence, and acid reflux, which are already common during pregnancy due to the hormone progesterone slowing the digestive tract.
*   **Fluoride Levels:** Check that the water does not have excessively high fluoride levels, as moderate consumption is healthy for teeth, but extremely high concentrations should be avoided.
*   **Plastic Bottling:** To minimize exposure to microplastics and chemical leaching (such as BPA), opt for mineral water packaged in glass bottles, or ensure plastic bottles are stored in a cool, dark place away from direct sunlight.
