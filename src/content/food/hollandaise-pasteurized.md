## Safety Status: Generally Safe

Commercially prepared or homemade hollandaise sauce made with pasteurized eggs is **safe** to consume during pregnancy.

According to food safety guidelines from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, pasteurization is the primary method for eliminating the risk of foodborne pathogens, specifically **Salmonella enteritidis**, in egg-based sauces. Pasteurization heat-treats the eggs, destroying harmful bacteria while preserving their culinary emulsifying properties. 

Because of this, commercially packaged hollandaise sauces (found in jars, cans, or dry packets) are safe to eat, as they are pasteurized and shelf-stabilized during manufacturing.

## Nutritional Profile & Considerations

Hollandaise sauce is a rich, high-fat condiment:
*   **Saturated Fats & Calories:** Hollandaise is made primarily of butter and egg yolks, making it very high in saturated fat and sodium. If you are managing gestational hypertension, high cholesterol, or rapid weight gain, limit your portion sizes.
*   **Choline & Vitamin A:** Because it contains egg yolks, pasteurized hollandaise provides small amounts of choline (essential for fetal brain development) and vitamin A.

## Safe Usage & Kitchen Storage

To ensure the safety of pasteurized hollandaise in your home:
*   **Check Store-Bought Labels:** Ensure any refrigerated, pre-made hollandaise is labeled "pasteurized." Dry mix packets and shelf-stable jars are safe.
*   **Homemade Guidelines:** When making it at home, only use eggs that carry the red "P" stamp on the shell or pasteurized liquid egg yolks. Do not use regular raw eggs.
*   **Refrigerate Leftovers:** Store opened commercial hollandaise in the refrigerator at or below **40°F (4°C)**. Consume within 3 days.
*   **Do Not Leave at Room Temp:** Discard any hollandaise sauce that has sat at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
