## Safety Status: Safe (When Fully Cooked)

Cooked shrimp is classified under the **safe** category and is one of the most popular, low-mercury shellfish options for pregnant individuals. 

The joint advisory by the FDA and EPA lists shrimp as a "Best Choice" seafood during pregnancy. Because shrimp feed low on the food chain and have short life spans, they do not accumulate dangerous levels of heavy metals. 

To maintain this safety status, shrimp must be **fully cooked**. Raw or undercooked shrimp can carry harmful pathogens, including **Vibrio** bacteria, **Salmonella**, and **norovirus**, which can cause severe food poisoning, severe dehydration, and maternal distress. Acid-cured preparations like ceviche are also unsafe because the acid does not cook the meat enough to destroy all potential pathogens.

## Nutritional Profile: Lean Protein and Iodine

Shrimp is highly nutritious and offers key benefits for prenatal health:
*   **Lean Protein:** A single serving of shrimp provides a significant amount of high-quality protein, which is essential for supporting the cellular growth of the fetus and the expansion of maternal blood volume.
*   **Iodine:** Iodine is critical for maternal thyroid hormone production, which directly supports the development of the fetal brain and nervous system.
*   **Selenium and Zinc:** Powerful antioxidants that support immune function and help protect tissues from oxidative damage.
*   **Low Fat & Calorie-Friendly:** Provides healthy fats (including small amounts of Omega-3s) without being calorically dense.

## Safe Selection, Prep & Kitchen Hygiene

To ensure food safety when preparing and consuming shrimp:
*   **Thorough Cooking:** Cook shrimp until they turn an opaque, pearly white or pink color and curl into a "C" shape. If they curl tightly into an "O" shape, they may be overcooked. Ensure they reach an internal temperature of **145°F (63°C)**.
*   **Thawing Safety:** Never thaw frozen shrimp on the counter. Always thaw them overnight in the refrigerator or in a bowl of cold water, changing the water every 30 minutes.
*   **Inspect Freshness:** Fresh shrimp should smell clean and slightly salty, like the ocean. Avoid shrimp that smell of ammonia or have soft, slimy textures.
