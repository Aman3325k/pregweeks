## Safety Status: Avoid Unless Pasteurized & Cooked

Soft, mold-ripened cheeses like Camembert are classified under the **avoid** category during pregnancy unless they are confirmed to be pasteurized and cooked until steaming hot.

According to warnings from the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** and the **Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)**, soft cheeses made from unpasteurized raw milk are high-risk sources of **Listeria monocytogenes**. Listeria is a resilient bacterium that can survive and grow at refrigeration temperatures. 

Listeriosis (Listeria infection) during pregnancy is highly dangerous; it can lead to miscarriage, premature delivery, stillbirth, or severe systemic infections in the newborn. Due to the high moisture and low acidity of Camembert, any Listeria present can multiply rapidly, posing significant risks.

## Safe Alternatives & Cooking Rules

You can safely satisfy cheese cravings during pregnancy with these options:
*   **Bake Until Bubbling:** You can safely eat Camembert if it is cooked thoroughly. Bake the cheese in the oven until it is completely melted, hot, and bubbling throughout (reaching an internal temperature of **165°F/74°C**). This heating process destroys any Listeria bacteria.
*   **Choose Hard Cheeses:** Hard cheeses, such as Cheddar, Swiss, Gouda, and Parmesan, are safe to eat cold. Because they have low water content and high acidity, they do not support Listeria growth, even if raw milk was used in their production.
*   **Confirm Pasteurized Soft Cheeses:** Safe soft cheese alternatives include commercially pasteurized cream cheese, cottage cheese, ricotta, and mozzarella.

## Storage and Kitchen Safety

If you handle soft cheeses in your kitchen:
*   **Read the Ingredients:** Verify that the package states "made with pasteurized milk." Avoid any cheese that says "raw milk" or does not specify pasteurization.
*   **Avoid Cross-Contamination:** Wash your hands, cutting boards, knives, and countertops thoroughly after preparing soft cheeses to prevent transferring bacteria to other foods.
*   **Keep Your Fridge Cold:** Ensure your refrigerator temperature is set to **40°F (4°C)** or below to slow bacterial growth.
