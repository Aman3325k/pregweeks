## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

**Hibiscus tea** is classified as an unsafe beverage that must be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

While many herbal teas are safe, hibiscus (*Hibiscus sabdariffa*) carries significant risks of hormone disruption and uterine stimulation that make it dangerous for both maternal and fetal health.

Scientific research and traditional medicine warnings indicate that hibiscus is an **emmenagogue**, a substance that can stimulate blood flow in the pelvic area and uterus. 

By inducing uterine contractions, consuming hibiscus tea—particularly in the first trimester—can lead to vaginal bleeding, cramping, and an increased risk of **miscarriage** or **preterm labor** in later pregnancy.

## Hormonal and Fetal Risks

Beyond stimulating the uterus, animal studies have raised serious concerns about the developmental impact of hibiscus on the fetus:
*   **Hormonal Disruptions:** Consuming hibiscus has been linked to changes in maternal estrogen levels. Estrogen regulation is crucial for maintaining a healthy pregnancy.
*   **Fetal Development Issues:** Offspring of maternal subjects exposed to hibiscus during pregnancy showed higher rates of low birth weight, delayed puberty, and increased risk of metabolic issues later in life.
*   **Blood Pressure Drop:** Hibiscus is known to naturally lower blood pressure in adults. While this might seem beneficial, a sudden drop in maternal blood pressure can reduce blood flow to the placenta and compromise oxygen delivery to the fetus.

## Hidden Sources of Hibiscus

Many pregnant individuals accidentally consume hibiscus because it is a common ingredient in blended teas:
*   **Fruit and Berry Blends:** Hibiscus is the primary ingredient in many commercially available "fruit" or "berry" teas (such as passion fruit, wild berry, or raspberry blends) because of its red color and tart flavor.
*   **Iced Teas:** Many restaurant iced teas and "agua de Jamaica" are made from hibiscus flowers. Always ask about the ingredients before ordering red-colored herbal drinks.
*   **Pregnancy Blends:** Double-check the ingredients on any tea marketed as "pregnancy-safe" or "pregnancy blends," as low-quality formulations may still contain hibiscus.
