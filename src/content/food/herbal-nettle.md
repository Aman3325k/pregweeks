## Safety Status: Avoid Entirely

**Nettle tea** (also known as stinging nettle tea) is a herbal beverage that should be strictly **avoided** during pregnancy. 

While nettle (*Urtica dioica*) is known for being rich in iron, calcium, and vitamin K, its chemical properties make it unsafe for pregnant individuals due to its potential to stimulate uterine contractions and lead to premature delivery or miscarriage.

In traditional and clinical herbology, nettle is recognized as an **emmenagogue**—an herb that can stimulate pelvic circulation and induce menstruation. 

Consuming nettle root or stinging nettle leaf can trigger smooth muscle contractions in the uterus. Particularly in the first and second trimesters, this uterine activity can lead to cramping, spotting, and increased risk of miscarriage.

## Hidden Exposure in Pregnancy Blends

One of the biggest risks with nettle tea is accidental exposure:
*   **Pregnancy Tea Blends:** Because nettle is highly nutritious and high in iron, it is a very common ingredient in commercial "pregnancy teas" or "third-trimester tonics" designed to prepare the body for labor.
*   **Safety Advice:** You should not consume these blends unless they have been explicitly cleared by your OB-GYN or midwife. It is safer to get iron and calcium from prenatal vitamins and safe foods (like cooked spinach, pasteurized cheese, or cooked clams) rather than unregulated herbs.
*   **Check Ingredient Labels:** Always read the full ingredient list of any herbal tea before purchase. Look for "nettle," "stinging nettle," "nettle leaf," or "nettle root."

## Safe Herbal Alternatives

If you are looking for safe, mineral-rich, caffeine-free herbal beverages during pregnancy, choose:
*   **Rooibos Tea:** Naturally caffeine-free, rich in antioxidants, and safe throughout pregnancy.
*   **Peppermint Tea:** Safely relieves gas, bloating, and nausea.
*   **Ginger Tea:** Clinically supported to reduce morning sickness.
*   **Lemon Water:** Highly hydrating and provides a boost of vitamin C.
